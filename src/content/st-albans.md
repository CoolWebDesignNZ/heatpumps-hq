## Heat pump installation in St Albans homes, old and new

In St Albans the age of the house decides the heat pump plan. An older home needs capacity for the heat it loses; a new townhouse needs heat taken upstairs.

- **Timber villas and bungalows** lose heat up into tall rooms and down through suspended timber floors. We size for the room's volume and look at draughts and underfloor insulation. A wall unit or floor console heats the main room, and bedrooms off the hall need their own.
- **Brick homes from the middle of last century** usually have lower ceilings and more compact rooms. A lounge unit sized for the room, with a small unit for each bedroom you use, usually suits them.
- **Two and three storey townhouses**, a growing number of them where older houses once stood, are well insulated. One unit handles the living level, and the bedrooms upstairs need small units or a multi-room system.

St Albans is a large inner suburb north of the central city with all three on its streets, so two houses side by side can need completely different systems.

## Heat pumps for St Albans villas and bungalows

High ceilings, large sash windows and suspended timber floors make a villa lovely to live in and hard to heat. Warm air rises to the top of a tall room, and cold air comes up through the floor. To deal with that we:

- size the unit for the room's full volume, not just its floor area
- place it so the airflow reaches the seating area, rather than the ceiling
- consider a [floor console](/single-room-heat-pump-installation/) where a wall unit would sit awkwardly above a bay or fireplace

Pipework on a villa needs care too. We run it along side or rear walls in neat covers, and keep the street frontage clear.

We also look at draughts and underfloor insulation, since a well sealed villa needs far less heating.

## New townhouses in St Albans

A modern townhouse is the opposite problem. It's well insulated, open plan downstairs and divided into bedrooms upstairs, with little outdoor space. One unit usually handles the living level. Upstairs, small bedroom units or a compact [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) keep each room comfortable.

The outdoor units need careful placement on narrow side paths. Our [noise problems guide](/heat-pump-noise-problems-solutions/) covers what makes an outdoor unit loud and how to avoid it.
