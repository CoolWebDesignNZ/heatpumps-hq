## What separates the best heat pump installers

The best heat pump installers get the size and the position right. A well made heat pump can still leave a room cold, defrost constantly, annoy the neighbours or drip down the wall if either is wrong. EECA's [good practice guide for heat pump installation](https://www.eeca.govt.nz/assets/EECA-Resources/Good-Practice-Guide-Heat-Pump-Installation-March-2022.pdf) says many problems with poorly performing heat pumps are due to poor location of the units.

So when you compare heat pump installers in Christchurch, or anywhere near you, compare how they work, not only the price. These are the checks that matter.

## 1. A licensed electrical worker does the electrical work

EECA's guide says only a registered electrician can hard wire the heat pump units. The [Electrical Workers Registration Board](https://www.ewrb.govt.nz/for-consumers/about-licences/) advises asking to see the worker's licence ID, physical or digital, checking it hasn't expired and the photo matches, and searching the Electrical Workers Public Register to confirm the licence hasn't been suspended.

Ask the installer directly who does the electrical connection. Whether that person is on staff or a subcontractor matters less than whether they're licensed.

## 2. You get a Certificate of Compliance

Under the Electricity (Safety) Regulations 2010, the EWRB says your electrical worker must give you copies of certification documents, and a [Certificate of Compliance](https://www.ewrb.govt.nz/for-consumers/documentation-from-your-electrical-worker/) is required for fixed wiring work. It has to be issued as soon as possible and no later than 20 days after the work is finished. EECA's guide warns that electrical work without a Certificate of Compliance may affect your house insurance. Keep it with your house records.

## 3. The refrigerant is handled by a certified person

Installing a split system means connecting refrigerant pipework, pressure testing it, evacuating it and opening the refrigerant charge. EECA's guide says installers should hold an **Approved Filler Compliance Certificate** for handling refrigerants, follow the Australia and New Zealand Refrigerant Handling Code of Practice, and never release refrigerant into the atmosphere.

Ask: who handles the refrigerant, and what certificate do they hold? If an old unit is being removed, ask how its refrigerant will be recovered.

## 4. The installer sizes the heat pump on site

A heat pump should be sized from the room's heat loss at the local design temperature, which EECA's guide lists as minus 4°C for Christchurch and Selwyn. That needs someone to look at the room: its size and ceiling height, the windows and which way they face, the insulation, and how you use the space.

Be wary of a size chosen from floor area alone, or a quote given without a visit. Ask the installer how they worked out the size, and what output the unit gives at a cold outdoor temperature. Our [heat pump size guide](/heat-pump-sizing/) explains what a good answer sounds like.

## 5. The outdoor unit position has been thought through

A good installer will talk about where the outdoor unit goes before quoting. EECA's guide asks for clear airflow around it, service access, somewhere condensate can drain, distance from LPG bottles, and a position that won't disturb you or the neighbours. In Christchurch the District Plan controls heat pump noise at the neighbour's property. See our [boundary rules guide](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/).

## 6. The quote is written and specific

A good quote lists:

- the brand, model number and capacity of each unit
- where the indoor and outdoor units will go
- the pipe route and how it's finished
- how the outdoor unit is mounted
- the electrical work, including any new circuit or switchboard work
- removal and disposal of any old unit
- whether the price is fixed.

If a detail isn't on the quote, ask before you accept. Our [installation cost guide](/heat-pump-installation-cost-christchurch/) explains why each item moves the price.

## 7. The installer commissions and explains the heat pump

Before the installer leaves, EECA's guide expects the system to be leak tested, evacuated, charged correctly for the pipe length, and test run. The installer should also:

- show you how to use the controls and remote
- show you how to remove and clean the filters
- explain what to expect in very cold weather
- tell you the service requirements.

You should be handed the electrical Certificate of Compliance, a record of the commissioning data, the operating manual, the warranty and a contact for after-sales support.

## 8. The heat pump installer will look after it afterwards

Ask who services and repairs the unit once it's in, and whether they work on that brand. A yearly service keeps a heat pump running efficiently. Find out who you call if something goes wrong in the first winter.

## Questions to ask any heat pump installer

Put these to every installer who quotes, and compare the answers:

1. Who does the electrical connection, and can I see their licence?
2. Who handles the refrigerant, and what certificate do they hold?
3. How did you work out the size, and what does the unit put out at minus 4°C?
4. Where will the outdoor unit go, and how will it affect the neighbours?
5. Is the price fixed, and what does it include?
6. What paperwork will I get when the job is finished?
7. Who services it, and who do I call if there's a problem?
