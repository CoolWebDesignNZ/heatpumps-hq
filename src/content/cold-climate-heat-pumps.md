## Why cold weather changes the heat pump you need

A heat pump warms your house by pulling heat out of the outside air. The colder that air, the harder the job, and a frosty Canterbury night makes it harder in two ways.

- **The house needs more heat just when the air has less to give.** Some models keep their output up as it gets colder. Others drop off.
- **Frost forms on the outdoor coil.** Below 5.5°C outside, the unit has to deal with frost. It has to use some of its heating power to defrost the coil.

A cold climate heat pump copes with both. It keeps enough output to hold the room warm on the coldest mornings. And it defrosts without leaving the room cold for long.

## What to look for in a cold climate heat pump

Check these three figures for the exact model, not just the brand.

### The 2°C heating figure on the label

New heat pump models sold here carry the Zoned Energy Rating Label. It shows the heating output at two outdoor temperatures, 7°C and 2°C. If your winter nights get down to 2°C or lower, check the 2°C figure is the same as the 7°C one, or higher. Use it to compare models. Some units don't cope well with frost. They give less heat at 2°C than at 7°C.

### Heating stars for the Cold zone

On the label, all of New Zealand is in the Cold zone. The heating stars there show how efficient the unit is against others of a similar size. More stars means less power for the same heat. Our [energy rating label guide](/energy-star-heat-pumps/) goes through every part of the label.

### The heating operating range

Every model has a lowest outdoor temperature it will keep heating at. It varies a lot from model to model. So check the exact model, not just the brand. Some specs also give a lower temperature that the unit keeps full rated output down to. Note the gap between those two numbers. Running at a temperature isn't the same as giving full output at it.

### A base heater or de-icer

Some outdoor units have a heater in the drain tray. It stops ice building up in the base. It doesn't add heat to the room. It's handy where frosts are heavy and regular.

## Sizing a heat pump for the coldest mornings

When a heat pump struggles in winter, it's rarely the brand. More often the unit was picked for an average day, not a cold one. We size from the rooms, insulation, windows and how you use the house. Then we check the unit's output when it's cold outside. Not just its headline kW.

This matters most in rooms with high ceilings, lots of glass, open stairwells or poor insulation. On a frosty morning those rooms lose far more heat than on a mild day. The [heat pump sizing guide](/heat-pump-sizing/) shows how the sums work.

## Placing the heat pump outdoor unit for frost

Where the outdoor unit sits changes how it copes with the cold.

- **Up off the ground.** Feet or a wall bracket keep the coil clear of ground frost. Ice can fall away from the base.
- **A clear drain path.** Defrosting makes meltwater. It needs to drain to garden or gravel. Not onto a path, deck or driveway where it will freeze.
- **Away from drips and drifts.** Under a roof edge with no spouting, the unit gets dripped on and ices up. Keep it clear of where snow slides off the roof.
- **Open air around it.** Boxed in by fences or a tight cover, it breathes its own cold air back in. That means more frost and more defrosting. Our [heat pump covers guide](/best-heat-pump-covers-nz/) shows screens that leave enough room.

## Cold climate heat pumps across Christchurch and Selwyn

Frosts are usually harder out on the plains than near the coast. Where your house sits changes what to plan for.

- **The plains.** In [Lincoln](/lincoln/) and [Templeton](/templeton/), still, clear nights bring hard frosts. Size for a frosty morning. Keep the outdoor unit out of spots where frost hangs around longest, like a shady corner against a south wall.
- **Low ground.** Cold air settles on the flat around [Tai Tapu](/tai-tapu/), and still nights by the estuary in [Bexley](/bexley/) bring frost and fog. Raise the unit on feet or a stand so meltwater runs away.
- **The hills.** In [Kennedys Bush](/kennedys-bush/), wind across the hillside strips heat faster, and the outdoor unit defrosts often on frosty mornings. Keep it out of the main wind where you can.
- **The coast.** Beach-side homes in [South New Brighton](/south-new-brighton/) take the full easterly. Wind can matter as much as frost there, so the outdoor unit goes on the sheltered side of the house.
- **Rural blocks.** On [McLeans Island](/mcleans-island/), the outdoor unit should sit out of the prevailing nor'west, on a raised base clear of long grass.
