## What a high wall heat pump is and who it suits

It's the indoor unit most people picture. A long, slim box mounted high on the wall, joined by pipes to an outdoor unit outside. It pulls air in through the top, heats or cools it, and blows it out across the room.

It suits most modern rooms with standard ceilings, where warm air doesn't get stuck up high: open plan living areas, bedrooms, studios and home offices. It also suits you if you want good cooling on a hot afternoon, or if you'd rather keep the floor clear for furniture.

## Where a wall mounted heat pump goes in the room

The best wall is usually the one that lets the unit blow along the longest part of the room, towards where you sit. Air needs room to travel and mix before it reaches you.

- **Not over the couch or the bed.** You'd feel a draught, and the air would hit you before it mixes.
- **Not above a window.** Cold glass drags the warm air down and away.
- **Clear of tall furniture.** A bookcase or wardrobe in front of the unit stops the airflow.
- **Near an outside wall if possible.** A short, straight pipe run is quieter, tidier and costs less.

In a bedroom, we aim the airflow along the room, not at the pillow.

## High wall heat pump or floor console

Both heat and cool. They move air differently, and that changes which one suits your room.

- **Heating evenness.** A console puts warmth out at floor level and lets it rise. A wall unit blows it down from above. In a room with a standard ceiling, both heat evenly. Under a tall stud, the console usually does better.
- **Cooling spread.** Cool air sinks. From high on the wall, it falls through the whole room. From a console, it tends to pool low.
- **Wall space.** A wall unit needs a clear stretch high up. A console needs a clear stretch low down, often under a window.
- **Windows.** Rooms that are mostly glass can leave no good spot up high. That's where a console under the sill earns its place.
- **Furniture.** A wall unit keeps the floor free. A console needs open space in front of it, so no couch pushed up close.
- **Filter access.** Console filters are at knee height. A wall unit's filters need a step stool.
- **Noise at head height.** A wall unit runs above you, and a console runs beside you. On a low fan, both are quiet. In a bedroom, a wall unit keeps the fan sound away from the bed.

If the room points to a console, read about [floor mounted heat pumps](/floor-mounted-heat-pumps/).

## What changes the price of a wall hung heat pump

The unit is only part of the price. The rest comes down to your house.

- **The size of unit.** A big or cold room needs more heat output, and a bigger unit costs more.
- **Outside or inside wall.** On an outside wall the pipes go straight through to the outdoor unit. On an inside wall they have to run through the ceiling or under the floor first.
- **How far apart the units are.** A longer run means more pipe, insulation and cover on the outside of the house.
- **How the outdoor unit is mounted.** Feet on flat ground are simplest. A wall bracket, a deck or a steep section takes more time and gear.
- **The switchboard.** If there's no room for a new circuit, the board needs work first.

## Replacing an old high wall heat pump

Old wall units often went where they were easiest to fit, not where they heat best. So before we swap like for like, we check two things. Is the unit the right size for the room? Is it on the right wall?

If both are fine, we can often reuse the hole in the wall and sometimes the pipe route. A newer unit may need a different pipe size or its own circuit, and we'll tell you before you agree to anything. We take the old indoor and outdoor units away.
