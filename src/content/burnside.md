## Family homes in Burnside

Burnside is an established north-western suburb of family homes, many of them brick and tile or weatherboard houses on decent sized sections, with plenty of larger homes among them. Over the years many have been added to: a new family room, a master suite, a converted garage. That often leaves a house where the original heating covers only part of the floor plan.

## Heat pump installation in an extended Burnside home

When a home has been extended, we look at it as a set of zones rather than one space. Typically that's the original living area, the newer family room, and the bedrooms. Each zone has its own size, windows and insulation, and the newer parts are usually better insulated than the old.

From there the choices are:

- **Separate units per zone.** Each sized for its room, each controlled on its own.
- **A [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/).** Several indoor units on one outdoor unit, which saves wall space outside.
- **Ducted with zones.** One system, vents in each room, and control over which areas are heated. Our [zoning page](/heat-pump-zoning/) explains it.

## Heat pump repair or replacement in Burnside

If the existing unit is ageing, it's worth deciding now whether to keep it and add to it, or replace it with a system planned around the whole house. We'll [diagnose](/heat-pump-repair/) the old unit honestly and price both routes.

## Heat pump service for busy Burnside households

Bigger households run their heat pumps hard. Filters clog faster with more people and pets, and a dirty filter makes the unit work harder for less heat. Rinse the filters every few weeks in winter and book a professional clean once a year. See [maintenance and servicing](/maintenance-servicing/).

## Matching old and new parts of a Burnside house

An original lounge and a new family room can feel very different on a winter evening. The older room loses heat through its walls and windows faster, so it often needs a slightly larger unit than its floor area suggests. Sizing each zone separately avoids one room being too warm while the other never quite gets there. More on how that works in our [sizing guide](/heat-pump-sizing/).
