## Normal heat pump noises

A healthy heat pump makes several normal noises, and knowing them saves you chasing a problem that isn't there:

- **A steady hum or whirr** from the outdoor unit while the compressor and fan run. It rises and falls as an inverter unit speeds up and slows down.
- **Air noise** from the indoor fan. Louder on high fan, quieter on low or auto.
- **A hiss or whoosh during defrost.** On frosty mornings the outdoor unit switches over to melt ice off its coil, then switches back. The indoor fan pauses while it does.
- **Clicks and creaks** from the indoor unit's casing as it heats and cools.
- **Gurgling or trickling** as refrigerant moves through the pipes, or as water drains from the coil.
- **A click from the outdoor unit** as relays and valves switch at start-up and shutdown.

If the noise is one of these and not unusually loud, the unit is doing its job.

## Heat pump noises that point to a problem

Each of these noises points to a short list of likely causes:

| Noise | Likely causes |
| --- | --- |
| Rattling | Loose panels or screws, a loose fan guard, debris in the fan, the unit moving on its feet or bracket |
| Buzzing | Vibration through the wall or bracket, a loose electrical connection, a failing fan motor or contactor |
| Grinding or scraping | Worn fan motor bearings, a fan blade catching on ice or a guard |
| High pitched squeal | Fan bearings or a motor on its way out |
| Banging or thumping | Ice hitting the fan, a loose compressor mounting, a unit that's not level |
| Constant gurgle with poor heating | Possible low refrigerant charge, which needs a leak check |
| Loud water drips or bubbling indoors | A blocked or poorly sloped condensate drain |

Buzzing with a burning smell, or any noise with smoke, means turn it off at the isolating switch and call. Don't keep running it.

## Fixing a noisy heat pump outdoor unit

Noise from the outdoor unit is the kind neighbours notice, so it's worth fixing first. The usual fixes:

- **Tighten and secure.** Panels, screws, the fan guard and the electrical cover. A rattle is often just a loose screw.
- **Anti-vibration feet or mounts.** Rubber feet under a ground unit, or isolation mounts on a wall bracket, stop the hum travelling into the house and the ground.
- **Re-level the unit.** A unit that has sunk on one side vibrates more and can let water pool in the base.
- **Clear ice and debris.** Leaves, twigs and built-up ice can catch the fan. Clear the base drain so meltwater doesn't refreeze.
- **Service the fan and coil.** A dirty coil makes the fan and compressor work harder. Worn fan bearings need replacing, not oiling.
- **Use quiet or night mode.** Some units have a setting that limits fan and compressor speed overnight.
- **Move it.** Away from bedrooms, out of corners where sound bounces, and off thin walls that act like a drum.

A screen can hide the unit and soften some sound, but it needs open space around it. See our [heat pump covers guide](/best-heat-pump-covers-nz/).

## Fixing a noisy indoor unit

Start with these checks and fixes when the noise comes from inside:

- **Clean the filters.** Clogged filters make the fan work harder and whistle.
- **Check the louvres.** Switching the unit off at the isolating switch for a few minutes sometimes clears a louvre that ticks or sticks. If it keeps happening, the louvre motor may need replacing.
- **Clean the fan wheel.** A dirty or unbalanced barrel fan hums and wobbles. That needs a [professional clean](/heat-pump-cleaning/).
- **Fix the drain.** Bubbling and gurgling from the indoor unit often mean the condensate drain is blocked or has no fall.
- **Check the mounting.** An indoor unit that isn't firmly fixed to its back plate can buzz against the wall.

## What the energy label says about heat pump noise

The Zoned Energy Rating Label can show indoor and outdoor sound levels. EECA notes that showing noise is optional in New Zealand, so some labels have it and some don't. The figures are measured at full capacity on cooling, so for much of the time a unit runs quieter than its label figure. For comparison, EECA puts a quiet home at about 30 dBA and a typical conversation at about 60 dBA ([EECA, Zoned Energy Rating Label guide](https://www.eeca.govt.nz/assets/EECA-Resources/Product-regulations/The-new-Zoned-Energy-Rating-Label-for-heat-pumps-air-conditioners.pdf)).

If you're buying a new heat pump for a spot near a bedroom or a neighbour, compare the outdoor sound figure between models.

## Noise and the neighbours in Christchurch

In Christchurch, there is no fixed distance a heat pump has to be from the boundary. What counts is the noise level at the neighbour's property, and the night-time limit is the one to watch.
