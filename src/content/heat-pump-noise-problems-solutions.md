## Normal heat pump noises

A healthy heat pump makes a few normal noises. Know them and you won't go hunting for a problem that isn't there:

- **Hum or whirr:** from the outdoor unit while the compressor and fan run. On an inverter unit it rises and falls as it speeds up and slows down.
- **Air noise:** from the indoor fan. It's louder on high, quieter on low or auto.
- **Hiss or whoosh:** during defrost on frosty mornings. The outdoor unit switches over to melt ice off its coil, then switches back. Meanwhile the indoor fan takes a break.
- **Clicks and creaks:** from the indoor unit's case as it heats up and cools down.
- **Gurgling or trickling:** gas moving through the pipes, or water draining off the coil.
- **A click outside:** relays and valves in the outdoor unit switching at start-up and shutdown.

If your noise is on that list and isn't unusually loud, the unit's doing its job.

## Heat pump noises that point to a problem

Each of these noises has a short list of likely causes:

| Noise | Likely causes |
| --- | --- |
| Rattling | Loose panels or screws, a loose fan guard, debris in the fan, the unit moving on its feet or bracket |
| Buzzing | Vibration through the wall or bracket, a loose electrical connection, a failing fan motor or contactor |
| Grinding or scraping | Worn fan motor bearings, a fan blade catching on ice or a guard |
| High pitched squeal | Fan bearings or a motor on its way out |
| Banging or thumping | Ice hitting the fan, a loose compressor mounting, a unit that's not level |
| Constant gurgle with poor heating | Possible low refrigerant charge, which needs a leak check |
| Loud water drips or bubbling indoors | A blocked or poorly sloped condensate drain |

Buzzing with a burning smell is serious. So is any noise with smoke. Switch it off at the isolator outside and ring us. Don't keep running it.

## Fixing a noisy heat pump outdoor unit

The neighbours hear the outdoor unit, so fix that first. Here's what usually works:

- **Tighten everything:** panels, screws, the fan guard and the electrical cover. A rattle is often just one loose screw.
- **Anti-vibration feet or mounts:** rubber feet under a unit on the ground, or isolation mounts on a wall bracket. They stop the hum travelling into the house and the ground.
- **Level it:** a unit that's sunk on one side shakes more. Water can pool in the base too.
- **Clear ice and debris:** leaves, twigs and built-up ice can catch the fan. Clear the base drain so meltwater doesn't freeze again.
- **Service the fan and coil:** a dirty coil makes the fan and compressor work harder. Worn fan bearings need replacing. Oiling won't fix them.
- **Quiet or night mode:** some units have a setting that keeps the fan and compressor slower overnight.
- **Move it:** away from bedrooms. Out of corners where sound bounces. Off thin walls that act like a drum.

A screen can hide the unit and soften some of the sound. It needs open space round it, though. Read our [heat pump covers guide](/best-heat-pump-covers-nz/).

## Fixing a noisy indoor unit

If the noise is inside, start here:

- **Filters:** clean them. Clogged filters make the fan work harder, and it whistles.
- **Louvres:** turn the unit off at the isolating switch for a few minutes. That sometimes clears a louvre that ticks or sticks. If it keeps happening, the louvre motor may need replacing.
- **Fan wheel:** a dirty or unbalanced barrel fan hums and wobbles. It needs a [professional clean](/heat-pump-cleaning/).
- **Drain:** bubbling and gurgling indoors often means the condensate drain is blocked or doesn't slope.
- **Mounting:** if the unit isn't fixed tight to its back plate, it can buzz against the wall.

## What the energy label says about heat pump noise

The Zoned Energy Rating Label can show indoor and outdoor sound levels. EECA notes that showing noise is optional in New Zealand. So some labels have it and some don't.

The figures are measured at full output on cool. Most of the time, a unit runs quieter than its label says. For comparison, EECA puts a quiet home at about 30 dBA and a normal conversation at about 60 dBA ([EECA, Zoned Energy Rating Label guide](https://www.eeca.govt.nz/assets/EECA-Resources/Product-regulations/The-new-Zoned-Energy-Rating-Label-for-heat-pumps-air-conditioners.pdf)).

## Noise and the neighbours in Christchurch

Christchurch has no set distance a heat pump must be from the boundary. What counts is how loud it is at the neighbour's place. The night-time limit is the one to watch.
