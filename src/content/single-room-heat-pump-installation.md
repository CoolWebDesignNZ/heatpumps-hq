## Single room heat pumps for Christchurch homes

One unit in one room sounds simple. In Christchurch, the house and the weather still change which unit we pick and where it goes.

- **Rental flats with a plug-in heater.** Flats cut out of villas in [Riccarton](/heat-pumps-riccarton/) and student rentals in [Ilam](/ilam/) often have nothing fixed in the living room. One high wall unit usually sorts it, sized to what the Healthy Homes heating standard needs for that room.
- **Shady rooms.** Houses in [Heathcote Valley](/heathcote-valley/) and by the river in [Beckenham](/beckenham/) get little winter sun. A shady room needs a bigger unit than a sunny room the same size, so we check which way your windows face before we size it.
- **Sunny rooms on the hills.** A north facing living area in [Mount Pleasant](/mount-pleasant/) or [Cashmere](/cashmere/) can be warm by lunchtime and cold once the sun drops. We size for the evening, and the same unit cools the room on a hot afternoon.
- **Frosty mornings out west.** In [Templeton](/templeton/) and [Avonhead](/avonhead/), still nights bring frost. We size for the coldest mornings and put the outdoor unit on raised feet or a bracket, on the most sheltered wall we can find close to the room.
- **Sleepouts and studios.** On lifestyle blocks in [Yaldhurst](/yaldhurst/) and [Lincoln](/lincoln/), a sleepout, studio or converted garage gets its own small unit and outdoor unit. That's simpler than running pipes across the section from the house.
- **Open plan new builds.** In [Prebbleton](/prebbleton/), a well insulated kitchen, dining and family room holds its heat, so one high wall unit sized for the whole floor area will warm it. Prebbleton and Lincoln come under the Selwyn District Plan, and the outdoor unit still has to stay quiet at the neighbour's place.

## When a single room heat pump is the right answer

A single room system suits a space you can shut off. It also suits one that's open to the rooms around it. Think lounge, open plan kitchen and dining, main bedroom, home office, sleepout or granny flat. It's also the usual fix for one cold room in a house that's otherwise fine.

It's not the answer for a row of separate bedrooms off a hall. Warm air from a wall unit stays in its own room. A heat pump in the lounge won't heat bedrooms with the doors shut.

## Sizing a small heat pump properly

Match the unit to the room. What counts is its heating kW rating. That's the heat it puts into the room. Too small, and it runs flat out on frosty mornings and never catches up. Too big, and it overshoots. It switches off, cools down and starts again. That's noisier, less comfortable and harder on the unit.

We size from your room, not a rule of thumb:

- **Floor area and ceiling height.** A raked or high ceiling adds air to heat, so the room needs more than its floor area suggests.
- **Insulation in the ceiling, walls and underfloor.** The less there is, the faster the heat leaves and the bigger the unit has to be.
- **Glass.** How much there is, whether it's double glazed and which way it faces. Single glazing and shady windows lose more heat.
- **Openings.** A room that opens to a stairwell, hall or another space loses warm air into it.
- **How you use it.** A bedroom heated overnight and a lounge heated from late afternoon work the unit at different times of day.

## Heat pumps for bedrooms

A small heat pump takes the chill off a bedroom at night. A warm room also gets less condensation on cold windows. That's a common problem in older Christchurch homes. Don't let the airflow blow straight onto the bed. Lots of people run a low fan overnight. So we check the unit we pick still holds the room's temperature on that setting.

## Choosing a wall unit or floor console

- **[High wall unit](/high-wall-heat-pumps/).** The usual choice. It sits high on the wall, out of the way. It pushes warm air across the room.
- **[Floor console](/floor-mounted-heat-pumps/).** A low unit at skirting height. It puts heat near the floor, where you feel it. It fits under a window. It suits rooms with no wall space up high. It does take up a bit of floor.

For a living area, we look at where you sit, where the windows and doors are, and where the pipes can run outside. Then we'll tell you which one.

## Placing the outdoor unit for a single room system

The outdoor unit usually goes on the wall right behind the indoor unit. The pipes then only have a short way to go. Does that wall face a neighbour's bedroom? Is it in a tight corner? Then we'll suggest a better spot and run the pipes further. The noise limits at the boundary are set out under [boundary rules](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/).
