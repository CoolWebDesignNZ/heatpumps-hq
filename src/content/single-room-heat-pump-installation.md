## When a single room heat pump is the right answer

A single room system suits a space you can shut off. It also suits one that's open to the rooms around it. Think lounge, open plan kitchen and dining, main bedroom, home office, sleepout or granny flat. It's also the usual fix for one cold room in a house that's otherwise fine.

It's not the answer for a row of separate bedrooms off a hall. Warm air from a wall unit stays in its own room. A heat pump in the lounge won't heat bedrooms with the doors shut.

## Sizing a small heat pump properly

Match the unit to the room. What counts is its heating kW rating. That's the heat it puts into the room. Too small, and it runs flat out on frosty mornings and never catches up. Too big, and it overshoots. It switches off, cools down and starts again. That's noisier, less comfortable and harder on the unit.

We size from your room, not a rule of thumb. We look at:

- floor area and ceiling height, including raked or high ceilings
- insulation in the ceiling, walls and underfloor
- how much glass there is, whether it's double glazed, and which way it faces
- whether the room opens to a stairwell, hall or another space
- how you use it, say a bedroom heated overnight or a lounge heated from late afternoon

Our [single room calculator](/calculator-single-room-heat-pump/) gives you a rough figure first. For how the numbers work, read about [heat pump sizing](/heat-pump-sizing/).

## Heat pumps for bedrooms

A small heat pump takes the chill off a bedroom at night. A warm room also gets less condensation on cold windows. That's a common problem in older Christchurch homes. Don't let the airflow blow straight onto the bed. Lots of people run a low fan overnight. So we check the unit we pick still holds the room's temperature on that setting.

## Choosing a wall unit or floor console

For one room, you're choosing between a high wall unit and a floor console.

- **High wall unit.** The usual choice. It sits high on the wall, out of the way. It pushes warm air across the room.
- **Floor console.** A low unit at skirting height. It puts heat near the floor, where you feel it. It fits under a window. It suits rooms with no wall space up high. It does take up a bit of floor.

For a living area, we look at where you sit, where the windows and doors are, and where the pipes can run outside. Then we'll tell you which one.

## Placing the outdoor unit for a single room system

The outdoor unit usually goes on the wall right behind the indoor unit. The pipes then only have a short way to go. Does that wall face a neighbour's bedroom? Is it in a tight corner? Then we'll suggest a better spot and run the pipes further. The noise limits at the boundary are set out under [boundary rules](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/).
