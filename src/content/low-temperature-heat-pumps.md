## The minimum temperature a heat pump will run at

There's no single answer. Every model has its own heating range. The maker sets the lowest outdoor temperature it will keep heating at. It's on the spec sheet for that model. Some Mitsubishi Electric models are rated to heat down to minus 25°C ([Mitsubishi Electric NZ](https://www.mitsubishi-electric.co.nz/publications/heat-pump/Heat-Pump-HyperCore-Technology-South-Island)).

That number is a limit. It's not a promise of full output. Two things happen as it gets colder, long before the unit hits that limit.

## What happens to a heat pump below zero

### Output drops

A heat pump moves heat from the outside air into the house. The colder that air, the more work each bit of heat takes. Some models hold their output well as it gets colder. Others give less heat on a frosty morning than on a mild afternoon. That's just when the house needs more. So size for cold mornings.

### Frost forms and the unit defrosts

EECA notes that below 5.5°C outside, a heat pump has to deal with frost. Some of its heating power goes into defrosting the coil ([EECA, Zoned Energy Rating Label guide](https://www.eeca.govt.nz/assets/EECA-Resources/Product-regulations/The-new-Zoned-Energy-Rating-Label-for-heat-pumps-air-conditioners.pdf)). While it defrosts, no heat goes to the room. You'll notice:

- the indoor fan slows or stops, and on some models a light comes on
- steam rises off the outdoor unit
- a hiss or whoosh as the refrigerant changes direction
- water drains out the bottom of the outdoor unit

After a few minutes it switches back. The warm air returns. On a cold, damp morning it can happen again and again. That's normal.

## Low temperature heat pump sizing in Christchurch

How cold your heat pump has to work depends on where the house sits.

- **The open plains.** In [Rolleston](/rolleston/) and [Templeton](/templeton/), clear winter nights bring hard frosts. That's when a heat pump works hardest. We size for the coldest mornings, not an average day.
- **Rural blocks with no shelter.** On [McLeans Island](/mcleans-island/), houses take the full weather coming across the plains. Wind pulls heat out of the walls and windows, and frost lowers the unit's output. We put the outdoor unit out of the nor'west and size for frosty mornings.
- **Exposed hillsides.** Up in [Kennedys Bush](/kennedys-bush/), wind across the hill strips heat faster than in the streets below, and the unit defrosts often on frosty mornings. A bracket or raised feet lets the melt water drain away.
- **Shady corners.** In [Lincoln](/lincoln/), we keep the outdoor unit out of spots where frost hangs around longest, like a shady corner against a south wall.

## When your heat pump is not working in cold weather

House still cold? Heat pump seems to be giving up? Go through these before you call.

1. **Check the mode and temperature.** Make sure it's on heat, not auto or dry. The set temperature needs to be above the room temperature.
2. **Clean the filters.** Clogged filters cut the airflow. Less air means less heat out of the indoor unit. It takes a few minutes.
3. **Look at the outdoor unit.** Is it buried in leaves, snow or ice? Is something blocking the back or sides? A wheelie bin, a woodpile or an overgrown hedge? Clear it.
4. **Watch for ice in the base.** If meltwater can't drain, it freezes again in the base tray and round the fan. Clear the drain path under the unit. Don't chip ice off the coil. The thin metal fins bend and puncture easily.
5. **Wait out a defrost.** If it's been off for a few minutes and the outdoor unit is steaming, let it finish.
6. **Reset it once.** Turn it off at the isolating switch beside the outdoor unit. Wait five minutes. Turn it back on.

Still not heating? Showing a flashing light or error code? Write down the code and call us. Some things are faults, not cold weather. Cool air for long stretches with no sign of a defrost. A coil that stays solid with ice. An outdoor fan that won't turn. See [heat pump repairs](/heat-pump-repair/).

## Why some heat pumps struggle more than others

Works fine in autumn but falls short every July? It's usually one of these.

- **It was sized for an average day.** It never had enough output for the room on a frosty morning.
- **It's an older or basic model.** It can't heat as low, or as hard, as a current cold climate model.
- **The outdoor unit is in a bad spot.** Boxed in, sitting in a frost hollow, or breathing its own cold air back in.
- **It needs a service.** A dirty outdoor coil or slightly low refrigerant tends to show up in the coldest weeks.

A service before winter rules out that last one. If the unit is too small or too old, we'll tell you what makes more sense. That could be a second unit, a bigger one or a replacement. For what to look for in a new one, see our [cold climate heat pump guide](/cold-climate-heat-pumps/).

## Heat pump settings for low temperatures and cold snaps

In a cold snap, keep the unit ticking over. Put the heat only where you need it.

- **Keep it running steadily.** Starting from a cold house at dawn asks the most of it when it has the least to give.
- **Use the timer.** Have it start before you get up. Don't wait till you're cold and then crank it up.
- **Leave the fan on auto.** Then it can push more air when it needs to put out more heat.
- **Shut doors to rooms you aren't heating.** The heat stays where you want it.
