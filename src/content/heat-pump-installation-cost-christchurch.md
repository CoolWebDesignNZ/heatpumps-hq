## Why we don't list heat pump prices

We don't publish heat pump prices or price ranges. A published figure can't allow for your house. And the house sets the cost of an install as much as the heat pump does. A free quote on site covers your job.

Knowing what moves the price also tells you what to look for in any quote, ours included.

## Heat pump installation cost for Christchurch homes

Your suburb and your type of house change the install work, so they change the price.

- **Steep hill sections.** On [Mount Pleasant](/mount-pleasant/) and in [Heathcote Valley](/heathcote-valley/), the outdoor unit often goes on a wall bracket or a deck, and some sites are up long flights of steps. We plan the bracket, the pipe route and the access on the site visit, so they're in the quote.
- **Apartments and unit-titled townhouses.** In the [Central City](/central-business-district-cbd/), the body corporate rules can decide where the outdoor unit goes and how the pipes run. Find out what yours allows before we quote a final layout.
- **Tight townhouse sites near the water.** In [Ferrymead](/ferrymead/), a townhouse outdoor unit may only fit in the courtyard, on a side wall, on a balcony or on the garage roof. Low sections can collect surface water, so the unit goes up on feet or a stand.
- **New builds before the linings go on.** In a new [Belfast](/belfast/) home, pipes for wall units can run inside the walls instead of in covers outside, and ducting gets a clear path through the ceiling. That's simpler work than fitting around finished walls and ceilings.

## How the unit affects heat pump installation cost

Three things about the heat pump itself change the cost.

**Size.** A bigger unit costs more than a smaller one in the same range. Getting the size right means you don't pay for capacity you don't need. It also means you don't end up with a unit too small to keep up. So we work out the [right heat pump size](/heat-pump-sizing/) before we price the job.

**Type.** High wall units, floor consoles, ceiling cassettes and multi split systems are priced differently. The install work differs too. So the [type of heat pump](/types-of-heat-pumps/) that suits your rooms shapes the quote.

**Model and range.** Brands sell more than one range. Higher ranges may add better output in the cold, quieter running, better efficiency or built-in Wi-Fi. Compare models of the same kW size on their energy labels.

## Heat pump install price for a wall unit or floor console

A wall unit and a floor console can cost different amounts to fit. That's because their pipes leave the room in different places. With a high wall unit, the pipes usually go straight out through the wall behind it. A floor console sits low. Its pipes may go down through the floor and under the house. Or they go out through the wall at skirting height. On a concrete slab with no crawl space, the pipes have to go through the wall or up and over. That can mean more pipe and more cover.

## Back-to-back or a longer pipe run

The simplest install is **back-to-back**. The indoor unit goes on an outside wall, with the outdoor unit right behind it on the other side. The pipes go straight through, and the run is short.

The price climbs as the run gets longer or harder:

- **An internal wall.** The indoor unit is on an inside wall, so the pipes have to cross a ceiling or the underfloor.
- **A corner.** The outdoor unit has to go round a corner or to the back of the house.
- **A tidy finish.** The pipes need a cover or ducting on the outside wall.
- **A tricky route.** The run goes through a garage, a second storey or a finished ceiling.

A longer run means more copper pipe, more insulation and more labour. There are some rules too. The run must stay within the maker's maximum length and height. Bends should be kept to a minimum. And the system must be charged with refrigerant to suit the pipe length.

## How outdoor unit mounting changes the cost

Where the outdoor unit goes changes the cost. A pad on the ground beside the house is usually simplest. Wall brackets add hardware and time. A two-storey wall adds access work. The common mounts are a concrete pad or pre-cast slab, a concrete patio, a timber deck with anti-vibration mounts, or brackets fixed to a wall or foundation. A roof is allowed only where the install has been designed for the load and wind forces.

Wherever it goes, the unit has to sit clear of obstructions. It has to be away from the neighbour's bedrooms. And defrost water has to be able to drain. The [heat pump boundary rules](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/) cover the noise side.

## Electrical work and the switchboard

Every heat pump needs an electrical connection by a licensed electrical worker. We have that done on every install. The price depends on the switchboard and the cable run:

- **Switchboard space.** Is there room for a new circuit, or does the board need upgrading?
- **Cable length.** How far does the cable run from the board to the outdoor unit?
- **The isolating switch.** It must be lockable. It goes on the house within reach for servicing, not on the unit itself.
- **Older wiring.** It may need work before a new load goes on.

The heat pump should be on its own circuit, wired back to the distribution board. An existing circuit with enough spare capacity is the other option. You should get an electrical Certificate of Compliance for the work.

## Removing an old heat pump

Replacing an old heat pump means taking it out first. We recover the refrigerant and disconnect the power. We remove both units and the old pipes. Then we patch the hole in the wall, or reuse it. Refrigerant must not be released into the air. So recovery is part of the job, not an optional extra.

Sometimes you can reuse the old position, which helps. Sometimes it was a poor spot to start with, and moving it is worth the extra.

## Access and the house itself

Access and how the house is built add smaller costs, and they add up. A weatherboard wall is different from brick or block. Framing may need extra dwangs to fix into. The underfloor space may be cramped. A unit high up a stairwell may need scaffolding or a tower. We look at all of this on the site visit. That way it's in the quote.

## Comparing heat pump quotes fairly

Got more than one quote? Check each one names the brand, model number and kW size, so you know you're comparing the same unit. Each should also say where both units go, how the pipes run and are finished, how the outdoor unit is mounted, and what electrical work is in: a new circuit, the isolating switch and any switchboard work. If an old unit is coming out, refrigerant recovery should be included. And ask whether the size was measured on site or guessed from the room size.

If one quote is much lower than the rest, check whether it's missing one of these lines.
