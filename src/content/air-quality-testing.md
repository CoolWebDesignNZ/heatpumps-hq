## What our indoor air quality testing covers, and what it doesn't

A formal indoor air quality test samples the air and sends it to a lab. Or it measures pollutants with calibrated gear. **We don't do that.** We're heat pump specialists. We check the heat pump. Is it clean? Is it dry and draining? Could it be spreading dust or mould round the room?

Need lab results for a health concern, an insurance claim or a building dispute? Get a specialist who does air sampling. If the heat pump turns out to be part of the problem, we can clean or repair it.

## How a heat pump can affect the air in your home

An indoor unit pulls room air through its filters, over a coil and through a fan. Dust collects on all three. The coil gets damp whenever the unit cools or dries the air. So mould can grow on the fins, in the fan wheel and in the drain tray. Every time the unit runs, room air passes over that build-up. For anyone with [allergies or asthma](/allergy-asthma-solutions-heat-pumps/), that's what sets them off when the unit starts.

## Air quality testing in Christchurch homes

Where your house sits in Christchurch changes what we tend to find inside the unit.

- **Dust from trucks, busy roads and yards.** Homes beside the commercial and industrial land in [Bromley](/bromley/) get more fine dust. It fills the filters and settles on the coil faster. We check those first, and show you how to lift the front panel and get the filters out.
- **Damp near the river and the lagoon.** Homes close to the river in [Dallington](/dallington/) and near the lagoon in [Brooklands](/brooklands/) can feel damp in winter. We check the drain tray and look for condensation and mould in the room. Then you know whether the damp starts in the unit or in the house.
- **Paddock dust on the city edge.** In [Yaldhurst](/yaldhurst/), dust blows in off the paddocks and shingle driveways. It builds up on the indoor filters as well as the outdoor coil. Clean the filters more often, and book the yearly check before winter.

## What we look at during an air quality test at home

We check the heat pump part by part. Then the room around it.

1. **Filters.** How clogged they are, and whether they're damaged or missing. Dust that gets past a filter ends up on the coil.
2. **The indoor coil.** Dust, grime and mould on the fins. Room air passes over it every time the unit runs.
3. **The fan wheel.** The barrel fan inside the outlet. Mould often builds up here, and it's hard to clean yourself.
4. **The drain tray and line.** Standing water, slime and blockages. Water that sits there is where mould and sour smells start.
5. **The smell.** Musty, sour, burning or chemical. Each one points to a different cause.
6. **Damp in the room.** Condensation on windows. Mould on walls or ceilings near the unit. These tell us if the house has a damp problem beyond the heat pump.
7. **Ducted systems.** Return air filters, the grilles, and the ducting we can reach. On a ducted system the air from all of them reaches every room.

## What happens after the air quality testing

We tell you plainly what we found. If the unit is dirty, we clean it properly. We wrap the indoor unit and catch the run-off. That [heat pump cleaning](/heat-pump-cleaning/) gets to the coil, fan wheel and drain tray, not just the filters. Smell won't go away? See [heat pump deodorising](/heat-pump-deodorising/). If the drain is blocked or damaged, we clear or repair it.

## When the problem is the house, not the heat pump

A heat pump can only do so much in a damp house. Cold, damp, poorly aired rooms grow mould and dust mites, and the windows run with condensation. Deal with moisture where it starts. Extractor fans that vent outside in the bathroom, kitchen and laundry make the biggest difference.

If we see condensation and mould well away from the heat pump, we'll tell you. Dry mode can help in some conditions. See [heat pump dehumidification](/heat-pump-dehumidification/) for when it's worth using.

## Keeping the air cleaner between visits

Most of it comes down to clean filters, a yearly [service](/heat-pump-servicing/) that checks the coil and drain, and dealing with damp where it starts. After cooling or dry mode, run it on fan or heat for a while. That lets the coil dry out.
