## What our indoor air quality testing covers, and what it doesn't

A formal indoor air quality test samples the air and sends it to a lab. Or it measures pollutants with calibrated gear. **We don't do that.** We're heat pump specialists. We check the heat pump. Is it clean? Is it dry and draining? Could it be spreading dust or mould round the room?

Need lab results for a health concern, an insurance claim or a building dispute? Get a specialist who does air sampling. If the heat pump turns out to be part of the problem, we can clean or fix it.

## How a heat pump can affect the air in your home

An indoor unit pulls room air through its filters, over a coil and through a fan. Dust collects on all three. The coil gets damp whenever the unit cools or dries the air. So mould can grow on the fins, in the fan wheel and in the drain tray. Every time the unit runs, room air passes over that build-up. For anyone with [allergies or asthma](/allergy-asthma-solutions-heat-pumps/), that's what sets them off when the unit starts.

## What we look at during an air quality test at home

We check the heat pump part by part. Then the room around it.

1. **Filters.** How clogged they are. Whether they're damaged or missing.
2. **The indoor coil.** Dust, grime and mould on the fins.
3. **The fan wheel.** The barrel fan inside the outlet. Mould often builds up here, and it's hard to clean yourself.
4. **The drain tray and line.** Standing water, slime and blockages.
5. **The smell.** Musty, sour, burning or chemical. Each one points to a different cause.
6. **Damp in the room.** Condensation on windows. Mould on walls or ceilings near the unit. These tell us if the house has a damp problem beyond the heat pump.
7. **Ducted systems.** Return air filters, the grilles, and the ducting we can reach.

## When the problem is the house, not the heat pump

A heat pump can only do so much in a damp house. The Cardiac and Respiratory Foundation NZ notes that cold, damp, poorly aired homes breed mould, dust mites and condensation. These can set off breathing symptoms. It recommends getting rid of moisture where it starts, with extractor fans in the bathroom, kitchen and laundry ([Cardiac and Respiratory Foundation NZ](https://cardiacandrespiratory.org.nz/your-health/your-home-and-your-health/)).

If we see condensation and mould well away from the heat pump, we'll tell you. Dry mode can help in some conditions. See [heat pump dehumidification](/heat-pump-dehumidification/) for when it's worth using.

## Keeping the air cleaner between visits

Most of the work comes down to three things. Clean filters. A yearly [service](/heat-pump-servicing/) that checks the coil and drain. And dealing with damp where it starts. After cooling or dry mode, run it on fan or heat for a while. That lets the coil dry out.
