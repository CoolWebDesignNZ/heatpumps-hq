## How a heat pump thermostat works

A heat pump thermostat is a small temperature sensor called a thermistor. It measures the air being drawn back into the indoor unit. Then it checks that against the temperature you set on the remote. The unit speeds up or slows down to close the gap. As the room gets close, a modern inverter unit drops to a low, steady output. It doesn't keep switching off and on.

So the unit only knows how warm the air is at its own intake. On a wall-mounted unit, that's high up near the ceiling. And that's where the warmest air in the room collects.

## Why the room feels colder than the setting

The unit reads the air at its intake. Where you sit can be several degrees cooler than the number on the remote. Here's why that happens:

- **Warm air near the ceiling.** High or raking ceilings, or a stairwell nearby, keep the air at the unit warmer than the air at couch height. The unit thinks its job is done.
- **Sun or heat near the unit.** Afternoon sun on the wall, or a TV underneath, warms the intake air. That fools the sensor.
- **Cold draughts.** An open door to a cold hallway, or single glazing, cools the room faster than the sensor notices.
- **Louvres pointing up.** Warm air never reaches the floor. In heat mode, point them down.

## When your heat pump is not reaching temperature

Sometimes the reading's right but the room still never warms up. Then the heat pump is not reaching temperature for one of these reasons:

- **Dirty filters or coils.** They cut the airflow and the heat output.
- **Too small a unit.** It may be undersized for the room. Or it's trying to heat a whole open plan floor it was never sized for. Output has to be [matched to the space](/heat-pump-sizing/) it heats.
- **Very cold mornings.** Every air source heat pump puts out less heat as it gets colder outside. It also pauses to defrost.
- **Low refrigerant.** A slow leak can cause it. We track that down through [leak detection and repair](/leak-detection-repair/).
- **A failed or drifted sensor.** A faulty thermistor can make the unit stop early, run on too long or show a fault.

## What our heat pump thermostat and temperature sensor check includes

Our check has five steps. It starts with a reading at seated height. It ends with whether the unit can heat the space at all.

1. **Measure the room.** We take the temperature at seated height with a calibrated thermometer. Then we compare it with what the unit reads.
2. **Test the sensors.** We test each thermistor against the resistance values in the maker's service information. If one reads out of range, we replace it.
3. **Check the settings.** Mode, fan speed, louvre direction and any temperature limits set in the controller.
4. **Check where the controller is.** On ducted systems and units with a wired controller, we look at where it's mounted. We check whether it's set to read the room or the return air.
5. **Check the output.** Filters, coils and refrigerant readings. That tells us whether the unit can heat the space at all.

You get a straight answer on which one is causing the problem. And you get a fixed price for anything that needs fixing.

## Getting the heat pump thermostat setting right

In winter, use Heat mode. Pick one steady temperature. Leave the fan on auto.

- **Heat, not Auto:** in winter, choose **Heat** rather than **Auto**. That stops the unit swapping to cooling when the room overshoots.
- **One temperature:** pick it and leave it. Nudging it up and down makes the unit work harder for nothing.
- **Fan on auto:** leave it there unless the room needs a stronger push of air.
- **Different rooms, different needs:** if one setting never suits both the lounge and the bedrooms, separate control may help. See [heat pump zoning](/heat-pump-zoning/).

With Wi-Fi controls on [smart heat pump systems](/smart-heat-pump-systems/), your phone shows the temperature the unit is reading. That makes a mismatch easier to spot.
