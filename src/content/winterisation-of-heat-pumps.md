## How to prepare your heat pump for winter

Do these in autumn, while it's still mild.

1. **Wash the filters.** Summer dust and pollen sit in them all season.
2. **Run it on heat for half an hour.** Find out it works before you need it.
3. **Check round the outdoor unit.** Clear leaves and weeds from under and around it. Cut back plants so air can move.
4. **Look at the feet or bracket.** The unit should sit off the ground. Then defrost water can drain, and the base won't freeze into a block of ice.
5. **Book a check if something seems off.** An autumn [tune-up](/heat-pump-seasonal-tune-ups/) sorts small problems before the cold.

## Heat pump winter settings that work

Heat mode. A steady temperature. Fan on auto. Louvres down. A timer. Doors shut.

**Use heat mode, not auto.** On a mild winter afternoon, auto can flick between heating and cooling. Heat mode keeps it on one job.

**Pick a steady temperature.** Setting it really high won't warm the room much faster. The unit just works hard for longer, and the room gets too hot. Pick a comfy number and leave it.

**Fan on auto.** The unit works out the airflow it needs. Want the room warm fast? Turn the fan up. It pushes more warm air into the room.

**Louvres down.** Warm air rises. Aim it at the floor and it spreads up through the room.

**Use the timer.** Have it come on before you wake up or get home. Starting from cold at full demand is harder on the unit than running gently.

**Shut doors to rooms you aren't heating.** A heat pump sized for the living area can't heat the whole house as well. Keep the warmth where you need it. Need more rooms heated? See [multi-room installation](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/).

## The outdoor unit in a Canterbury winter

Over winter the outdoor unit copes with frost, meltwater, ice and the odd snowfall. Each one needs handling a bit differently.

**Frost and defrost.** On a cold, clear night, damp in the air freezes on the outdoor coil. The unit runs backwards for a few minutes to melt it. You'll see steam and hear a whoosh. The indoor fan stops while it does this. The room may feel a bit cooler for a moment. That's normal.

**Where the meltwater goes.** Defrost water drips from the base. On a concrete path it can freeze overnight into a slippery patch. Is the unit above a path or doorway? Ask us about a drain tray or a gravel soak area.

**Ice that stays.** Ice getting thicker every night? Whole unit encased? Then the defrost isn't keeping up. It could be a sensor. It could be low refrigerant. Or the unit could be sitting in a frozen puddle. Turn it off and call us. Don't chip the ice off. The fins bend easily.

**Snow.** If snow settles on the unit, brush it gently off the top and away from the sides with a soft broom. Don't use hot water.

**Don't box it in.** A tight box or a tarp on a running unit blocks the air and traps defrost water. The safe kind of [heat pump cover](/covers/) only shields the top.

## Heat pump winter faults worth catching early

Call us about these before they get worse.

- Air from the indoor unit only lukewarm
- Outdoor unit running nonstop, but the room never warms up
- Ice that won't clear
- Steam or water coming from inside the house, not outside
- A new grinding or rattling noise when it starts

Some just need a clean. Some need a [repair](/heat-pump-repair/). An older unit that struggles every winter may be due for a [cold climate heat pump](/cold-climate-heat-pumps/) built to heat when it's freezing outside.
