## Heat pump leaking water or refrigerant?

These are two different problems with two different solutions.

- **Water** comes from condensation. When the indoor unit cools or dries the air, moisture forms on the cold coil. It drips into a tray and runs away down a drain hose. In heating mode, the outdoor unit makes the water instead, when it defrosts.
- **Refrigerant** is the gas sealed inside the copper pipes. It carries heat between the two units. It should never get out. If it does, the unit loses heating power and stops in the end.

## Heat pump leaking water inside

Water dripping from the indoor unit, or staining the wall below it? It's nearly always the drainage.

- **A blocked drain.** Dust, mould slime and the odd insect build up in the tray and hose. In the end, water can't get past.
- **A kinked, sagging or crushed hose.** Water sits in the low spot and backs up into the unit. It can happen where a hose runs inside a wall or behind a cover.
- **A unit that's dropped out of level.** The tray is made to drain one way. If the unit tilts the other way, water spills over the front.
- **A failed condensate pump.** Some installs can't drain by gravity, so they use a small pump. When it stops, the tray overflows.
- **Ice on the indoor coil.** Very dirty filters can let the coil freeze in cooling mode. When it thaws, more water arrives than the drain can take.

The repair is usually simple. We clear and flush the tray and hose. Or we re-route or replace the hose, or level the unit. Our [heat pump cleaning](/heat-pump-cleaning/) service deals with mould in the tray and coil.

## Heat pump dripping water from the outdoor unit

In heating mode, water dripping from the outdoor unit is defrost meltwater. That's meant to happen. You'll see a steady drip, or a short burst after the unit's been steaming. What matters is where the water goes. It shouldn't pool against the foundations. It shouldn't run across a path where it'll freeze, or drip onto the neighbour's side. A drain fitting with a hose to a gully solves that. So does raising the unit on feet over gravel.

## Heat pump leaking water in Christchurch homes

Where the water goes depends a lot on the house and the weather around it.

- **Frosty mornings.** Out west in [Hornby](/heat-pumps-hornby/) and [Wigram](/wigram/), clear winter nights bring hard frosts, and the outdoor unit lets off defrost water. We raise the unit so that water drains away instead of freezing under it or across a path.
- **Damp houses.** In [Shirley](/shirley/) and near the river in [Dallington](/dallington/), homes can feel damp in winter. A blocked drain can drip down the wall, so we check the drain at every service.
- **Townhouse balconies.** In a [Ferrymead](/ferrymead/) townhouse, the outdoor unit may end up on a balcony. Its defrost water can't just drip onto the unit below, so we give it somewhere to go.
- **Split level hill homes.** In [Heathcote Valley](/heathcote-valley/), the drain line may run under a floor or along a retaining wall. It needs a steady fall all the way to a drain or the garden. We plan that route at the quote.
- **Rentals.** A heat pump in an [Aranui](/aranui/) rental often gets little care between tenants, and the drain blocks. A regular service between tenancies heads that off.

## Heat pump refrigerant leak

A heat pump refrigerant leak is usually slow. The signs build up bit by bit. Watch for these:

- **Slower heating:** the room takes longer to warm up each week.
- **Oily marks:** residue around the pipe joints at either unit. Refrigerant carries a little oil with it.
- **Frost or ice:** on the pipe joints or valves, where it doesn't normally form.
- **Hissing:** a sound near the pipes.
- **A fault code:** the unit stops and shows one.

Leaks tend to start at joints. The flare joints where the pipes meet the units are the usual spot. Pipes rubbing on a bracket or wall can cause them too. So can corrosion on the outdoor coil.

## How we find and repair a refrigerant leak

1. **Pressure and temperature readings** tell us if the charge is low.
2. **Electronic leak detection and bubble testing** around the joints, valves and coils pinpoints where it's escaping.
3. **Pressure testing with nitrogen** finds a leak too small to show while the system runs.
4. **The repair.** Usually that's re-making a joint or replacing a length of pipe. If a coil is leaking, we price that separately. Then you can weigh it up against a new unit.
5. **Evacuating and recharging.** We pull the system into a vacuum to clear out air and moisture. Then we charge it by weight to the maker's figure.

Most heat pumps won't warn you about a refrigerant leak as it starts. You'll notice falling performance or a fault code instead.

## If you think you have a heat pump gas leak

Switch the heat pump off and open a window in the room. Keep naked flames and portable heaters away. Don't tighten the pipe nuts or try a repair kit. Over-tightened flare joints crack, and the leak gets worse.
