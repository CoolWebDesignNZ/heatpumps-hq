## Heat pump installation in Spreydon homes

Lots of Spreydon homes already have a heat pump. So the first question is often simple. Does the old unit still heat the house properly, or is it time to replace it? If you don't have one yet, the type of house decides which system suits.

- **Weatherboard and brick bungalows.** These have a closed lounge with bedrooms off a hall. They lose heat through the ceiling, the floor and older windows. A lounge unit won't push heat down the hall. So cold bedrooms need a small unit each, or a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/).
- **Former state houses.** They're compact, with a separate lounge and the bedrooms close together. The biggest thing for unit size is whether the ceiling and underfloor have been insulated since they were built. We check both before we quote.
- **Blocks of units and townhouses on redeveloped sections.** The living areas are smaller and often open plan. One high wall unit can heat them. The house next door is close. So the outdoor unit needs a spot away from the neighbour's windows.

Spreydon is a flat, established suburb south-west of the city centre. It runs towards the foot of the Port Hills. On flat sections, the outdoor unit usually has an easy spot at the side or back. We raise it on feet so defrost water drains away.

## Signs your Spreydon heat pump needs replacing

A heat pump at the end of its life usually shows it:

- **It can't keep the room warm** on cold nights, even after a clean.
- **The same faults keep coming back**, or a major part like the compressor has gone.
- **It's noisy or rattles**, and a service doesn't fix it.
- **Parts are hard to get** for the model.

Not sure? Start with a [service](/maintenance-servicing/). Clogged filters and dirty coils make plenty of good units seem worn out.

## What we check before quoting a replacement

A new heat pump is a chance to fix what wasn't right the first time, so we check all of it before we quote:

- **The room as it is now.** Insulation, new windows and renovations all change how much heat it needs.
- **Where the indoor unit sits.** If the old one blew into a corner or onto the couch, we'll suggest a better wall.
- **Where the outdoor unit sits.** Years ago, units sometimes went in a corner or right under a neighbour's bedroom. Moving it can make it quieter and more efficient.
- **Whether you need more than one room heated.** If the bedrooms are always cold, a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) may beat a straight swap.

## Spreydon homes without a heat pump yet

If you don't have a heat pump yet, we measure each room. We check how it's insulated and glazed. From that we pick the unit and where it goes. A floor console can suit a bungalow lounge with a low window across most of one wall. If the house is on piles, the pipes can often run under the floor and come up behind the unit. Then very little shows, inside or out.
