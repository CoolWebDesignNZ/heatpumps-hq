## Before we arrive

When you book, tell us how many indoor units you have, whether any are ducted, and roughly where the outdoor units sit. A unit on a second-storey bracket or under a deck changes the gear we bring. If something has been bothering you, such as a drip, a noise or a smell, mention it. We'll look at that first.

Service visits run Monday to Friday, 8am to 5pm, anywhere in Christchurch, Selwyn and Lyttelton.

## Step 1: listen and look

The unit is switched on and run before anything comes apart. We listen for rattles and bearing noise, watch the louvres move, and check the remote talks to the unit properly. A fault that only shows while it's running is easy to miss once the covers are off.

## Step 2: the indoor unit

1. **Power off at the isolator.** Nothing is cleaned while the unit is live.
2. **Filters out and washed.** The mesh filters are washed, dried and checked for tears. Any extra filters your model has, such as deodorising or allergen filters, are checked and replaced if needed.
3. **Coil cleaned.** The aluminium fins behind the filters are where dust and mould build up. We clean them with a coil cleaner and rinse them, catching the run-off.
4. **Fan wheel checked.** The long barrel fan inside the unit collects grime on its blades, which cuts airflow and spreads smells. We inspect it and clean it where it can be reached.
5. **Drain tray and drain line flushed.** Water is poured into the tray to prove it runs away freely. A slow drain is the usual cause of a unit dripping down the wall.

## Step 3: the outdoor unit

1. **Coil cleared.** Leaves, cobwebs, lint and grass clippings come off the fins so the unit can move air.
2. **Fan and guard checked.** We make sure the blade turns freely, isn't chipped and isn't rubbing.
3. **Base and drainage.** Defrost water has to drain away. We clear the base, check the drain holes and make sure the unit isn't sitting in a puddle or packed with leaves.
4. **Feet, brackets and pipe insulation.** Loose bolts, perished rubber feet and sun-damaged pipe lagging are noted, and fixed where it's a small job.

## Step 4: electrical connections

We check the isolating switch, the terminal connections and the interconnecting cable for heat damage, loose screws or water getting in. Any electrical repair is done by a licensed electrical worker.

## Step 5: the test run

With everything back together, the unit runs in heating and then cooling. We measure the air temperature going in and coming out of the indoor unit. A healthy system shows a clear difference between the two; a weak difference points to a refrigerant or airflow problem worth looking into. We also check the outdoor unit starts, ramps up and runs smoothly.

## Step 6: what you get

Before we leave, we tell you what we did and what we found, in plain words. If a part is worn, we explain what it is, whether it needs doing now or can wait, and what it would cost to replace. You decide. Nothing extra is done without your agreement.

## What sets the cost of a service

There's no single price, because no two homes are the same. These are the things that move it:

- **Number of units.** Each extra indoor unit adds time.
- **Type of system.** A [ducted system](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/) has a return air filter, ceiling grilles and an indoor unit in the roof space, which takes longer than a wall unit.
- **Access.** A high stairwell unit, an outdoor unit on a wall bracket or one squeezed behind a fence takes longer to reach safely.
- **Condition.** A unit that hasn't been touched in years may need a full [heat pump cleaning](/heat-pump-cleaning/) rather than a standard service.

We agree a fixed price with you before any work starts.
