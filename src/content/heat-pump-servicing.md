## What an annual heat pump service covers

- **Coil clean:** the dust and mould that builds up behind the filters
- **Filter deep clean,** not just a rinse
- **Drain flush,** so condensate doesn't back up or smell
- **Electrical check:** connections, draw and safety
- **Refrigerant pressure test,** to catch a slow leak before it becomes a repair

If something needs repairing, we'll tell you before we do it.

## Step 1: listen and look

We turn the unit on and run it before anything comes apart. We listen for rattles and bearing noise. We watch the louvres move. We check the remote talks to the unit. Some faults only show while it's running. They're easy to miss once the covers are off.

## Step 2: the indoor unit

The indoor unit gets the most time. Filters first, drain line last.

1. **Power off at the isolator.** We never clean a live unit.
2. **Filters out and washed.** We wash and dry the mesh filters and check for tears. Some models have extra filters, like deodorising or allergen ones. We check those and replace them if needed.
3. **Coil cleaned.** Dust and mould build up on the aluminium fins behind the filters. We clean them with coil cleaner and rinse them. We catch the run-off.
4. **Fan wheel checked.** The long barrel fan inside gets grime on its blades. That cuts airflow and spreads smells. We check it and clean what we can reach.
5. **Drain tray and line flushed.** We pour water into the tray to prove it drains freely. A slow drain is the usual reason a unit drips down the wall.

## Step 3: the outdoor unit

1. **Coil cleared.** Leaves, cobwebs, lint and grass clippings come off the fins. Then the unit can move air.
2. **Fan and guard checked.** We make sure the blade spins freely. No chips, no rubbing.
3. **Base and drainage.** Defrost water has to drain away. We clear the base and check the drain holes. We make sure the unit isn't sitting in a puddle or packed with leaves.
4. **Feet, brackets and pipe lagging.** We note loose bolts, perished rubber feet and sun-damaged lagging. Small jobs we sort on the spot.

## Step 4: electrical connections

We check the isolating switch, the terminals and the cable between the units. We're looking for heat damage, loose screws or water getting in. A licensed electrical worker does any electrical repair.

## Step 5: the test run

With it all back together, we run it in heating, then cooling. We measure the air going into the indoor unit and the air coming out. A healthy system shows a clear gap between the two. A small gap points to a refrigerant or airflow problem. We'd look into that. We also check the outdoor unit starts, speeds up and runs smoothly.

## Step 6: after the heat pump service

Before we leave, we tell you what we did and what we found. In plain words. If a part is worn, we tell you what it is, whether it needs doing now or can wait, and what it would cost to replace. You decide. We do nothing extra without your OK.

## What sets the cost of a heat pump service

There's no single price, because no two homes are the same.

- **How many units.** Each extra indoor unit adds time.
- **Type of system.** A [ducted system](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/) has a return air filter, ceiling grilles and an indoor unit up in the roof. It takes longer than a wall unit.
- **Access.** A unit high in a stairwell takes longer to reach safely. So does an outdoor unit on a wall bracket, or one squeezed behind a fence.
- **Condition.** A unit nobody's touched in years may need a full [heat pump cleaning](/heat-pump-cleaning/), not a standard service.

We go through all of these at the quote, and quotes are free.
