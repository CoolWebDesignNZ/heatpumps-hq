## How a multi room heat pump works

A multi split has one outdoor unit and several indoor units. People sometimes call them heads. Each indoor unit has its own pair of refrigerant pipes and a control cable back to the outdoor unit. Every room gets its own remote or wall controller. So you can warm the bedrooms at night and leave the lounge off.

The indoor units don't all have to match. On most systems, a bedroom can have a small high wall unit and the lounge a bigger one. A room with no high wall space can have a floor console.

## When a multi split heat pump makes sense

It makes sense if any of these fit your house.

- **Not much outside wall.** Townhouses, units and homes close to the boundary often have room for one outdoor unit, not three.
- **Noise near a neighbour.** One well placed outdoor unit is easier to keep quiet than several. The District Plan noise limits at the boundary are set out under [boundary rules](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/).
- **Looks.** One outdoor unit on the side of the house, not a row of them.
- **Rooms on different floors.** Upstairs bedrooms and a downstairs living area can share one outdoor unit. The pipes just have to be able to run between floors.

## The trade-offs of a multi split

A multi split isn't always the better choice. Know the downsides before you decide.

- **One point of failure.** If the outdoor unit stops, every room on it goes cold. With separate systems, a fault takes out one room.
- **Shared output.** The outdoor unit splits its output between the rooms that are on. Size it sensibly for how you use the house and it copes well. But that's one reason sizing matters more on a multi split.
- **Longer pipe runs.** Each indoor unit needs its own pipes back to the outdoor unit. Planning the routes through the roof, under the floor or along the outside wall takes more work.
- **Thinking ahead.** Adding a room later needs a spare port and spare capacity on the outdoor unit. Decide that up front.

## Multi split, separate units or ducted

For two or three rooms, we'll often price a multi split against separate single systems. Separate units are simpler to service. Each room runs on its own. It's worth seeing both on paper.

Want every room heated and nothing on the walls? A [ducted heat pump](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/) is the other way. Ducting needs ceiling or underfloor space. Room by room control comes from a [zoning system](/heat-pump-zoning/), not separate indoor units. For more detail, read [ducted vs split heat pumps](/ducted-vs-split-heat-pumps-christchurch/).
