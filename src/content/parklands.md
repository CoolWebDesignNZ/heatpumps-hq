## Parklands homes and heating

Parklands is a residential suburb of mostly single-storey homes on flat land between Burwood and the coast. A lot of the houses are brick and tile, built from the 1970s onward, and the later streets have newer homes with better insulation and double glazing.

Brick veneer homes from the 1970s and 80s sit in the middle. They usually have ceiling insulation and some wall insulation, but often original aluminium windows. They hold heat better than an older weatherboard villa, and lose more than a new build.

## Replacing a first heat pump

Many Parklands homes already have a heat pump that was fitted years ago. When one reaches the end of its life, it's a good time to ask whether it's still in the right place and the right size. An early install was sometimes sized by guesswork or placed where it was easiest to fit rather than where it heats best. When we replace a unit, we work the size out again from the rooms, windows and insulation, and move it if the old position was a poor one.

## Heating the whole house

The typical Parklands layout puts the lounge and kitchen at one end and the bedrooms down a hallway. To heat both, you can:

- Add a second wall unit in the hallway or main bedroom.
- Fit a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) with one outdoor unit.
- Go [ducted](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/), if the roof space allows, for even heat in every room.

## Outdoor units and neighbours

Parklands sections are flat and the houses are often close together, so the main question is noise. Christchurch limits it at the neighbour's boundary, especially at night. We place the outdoor unit away from their bedroom windows and out of tight corners, and mount it on anti-vibration feet.

Parklands is close enough to the coast for some salt to arrive on an easterly, so a gentle rinse of the outdoor coil now and then is worth doing.
