## Heat pump installation in Edgeware's mix of old and new homes

An Edgeware heat pump plan starts with one question: is the house an original cottage or villa, or one of the newer townhouses? The two lose heat in different places and suit different systems.

- **Timber cottages and villas** on narrow sections, many renovated and some still close to original, have high studs, sash windows and gaps around doors and floors. Heat rises to the ceiling and slips out through the draughts, and the rooms close off from one another, so a unit in the lounge seldom warms more than the lounge. They suit a wall unit or floor console placed to push air down to where you sit, plus a small unit in the main bedroom.
- **Townhouses in blocks of two or three**, built where older houses made way, are better insulated and usually split across two levels. Heating the living area downstairs is the easy part. The bedrooms upstairs are where the plan needs thought.

Edgeware is flat, close to the city and built tight, so the limit on most installs is outdoor space rather than terrain. At the same visit that we measure the rooms, we check where the outdoor unit can go and how close the next bedroom window is.

## Heating Edgeware villas and cottages

Heating an Edgeware villa or cottage well means dealing with the high stud and the draughts. Old timber homes have high studs, sash windows and gaps around doors and floors. Warm air rises to that high ceiling and leaks out through draughts. A few things change the result:

- **Place the indoor unit to push air down and across the room**, not straight at a ceiling rose or into a hallway.
- **Consider a floor console** under a window in a high-stud lounge. It puts warm air at the level you sit and suits walls that are mostly windows and doors.
- **Fix the obvious draughts.** Weather strips on doors and sashes are cheap compared with running a larger unit.
- **Heat the rooms you use.** A small unit in the main bedroom often matters more than oversizing the lounge unit to try to push heat down the hall.

Pipe runs in an old cottage can usually go through the underfloor, which keeps them off the front of the house.

## Edgeware townhouses and units

The newer townhouses in Edgeware are a different job. They're better insulated, often over two levels, with the living area downstairs and bedrooms above. A single wall unit in the open plan living area handles the ground floor. Bedrooms upstairs may need a small unit or two, or a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) that runs several indoor units from one outdoor unit, which saves wall space outside.

Outdoor space is tight. Side paths are narrow, and the next unit's bedroom may be a metre or two away, so we place the outdoor unit with noise in mind.
