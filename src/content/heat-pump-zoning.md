## The problem heat pump zoning solves

Without zoning, a ducted heat pump heats every room at once, whether anyone is in them or not. A ducted heat pump without zoning has one thermostat, usually on the wall controller, and it sends air to every outlet whenever it runs. Warm the lounge on a winter afternoon and you also warm the empty bedrooms. Turn it on for the bedrooms at night and the living areas get heated too.

Zoning splits the house into areas that can be switched and controlled separately, so the system works on the rooms you're in.

## How ducted heat pump zoning works

Ducted zoning works through motorised dampers in the ducts, a zone controller, temperature sensors on more advanced systems, and a way of handling the extra air when zones close:

- **Dampers.** Motorised dampers sit in the duct runs, either in the main ducts to each zone or in the duct to each room. They open to let air through or close to shut it off.
- **Zone controller.** A wall controller, and often a phone app, where you turn zones on and off and set times.
- **Temperature sensors.** Simpler systems switch zones on and off. More advanced ones have a sensor in each zone and adjust the damper or airflow to hold that zone's own set temperature.
- **Airflow management.** When several zones close, the system has to deal with the extra air, either by reducing the fan speed or by letting air bypass. A good design stops the open zones getting noisy.

## Zone on and off, or temperature in each zone

There are two levels of zoning, and the difference matters:

1. **On and off zoning.** You choose which zones get air. The heat pump still works to one thermostat, so the open zones all aim for the same temperature.
2. **Temperature control in each zone.** Each zone has its own sensor and set point. The lounge can sit warmer than the bedrooms while both are on.

## Planning the zones

The zones should follow how the house is used, not just its floor plan. Common splits are:

- living areas and kitchen in one zone, bedrooms in another
- a separate zone for a home office used during the day
- upstairs and downstairs as separate zones in a two storey home
- a guest room or rumpus room that stays shut off most of the time

The heat pump also needs enough air going somewhere when only one small zone is open. That affects how small a zone can be, and it is one reason zoning is best designed with the duct layout rather than added as an afterthought.

## Adding zone control to an existing ducted heat pump

Zone control can often be retrofitted to an existing ducted heat pump that heats the whole house. To tell whether yours can take it, we look at:

- whether the indoor unit works with the zone controller you want
- the duct layout, and whether each zone can be isolated with a damper
- the condition of the ducts, since leaking or crushed ducts undo the benefit
- where the controller and any zone sensors can go

If the system is old or the ducting is in poor shape, we may suggest replacing the system with zoning built in. We'll give you both options with a fixed price.

## Multi zone heating with other heat pump types

Zoning is a ducted system term. With wall units or floor consoles, each indoor unit already has its own control. That is why a [multi room heat pump](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) is sometimes the simpler answer when you want separate control of a few rooms and don't need everything hidden in the ceiling.
