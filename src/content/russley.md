## Heat pump installation in Russley's brick homes

Most Russley homes are brick and tile family houses from the 1960s to the 1980s. They have separate living rooms and a bedroom wing, and that layout decides the heat pump. Heat from one unit in the family room won't reach a bedroom wing down the hall. So one heat pump rarely heats the whole house well. Lots of owners started with a single unit in the family room. Now they want the bedrooms warm too.

- **Adding to a family room unit you already have.** Put a small, quiet wall unit in each bedroom you use. Or run the bedrooms from one new outdoor unit with a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/).
- **Heating the whole house.** Go [ducted](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/) if the ceiling space allows. You get a vent in each room and nothing on the walls.
- **A formal lounge you only use now and then.** Some homes have one. It gets its own small unit, or a zone you can close off. More on that below.
- **Bigger homes on bigger sections.** More rooms to cover and longer pipe runs. These more often go ducted or multi-room.

In homes this age, the ceiling insulation can be thin or settled. Single glazed windows lose heat on frosty nights. We check both at the quote, because they decide how big each unit needs to be. Russley sits beside Russley Road on the western edge of Christchurch. It's close to the airport and the golf course, and many homes are on quiet cul-de-sacs.

## Choosing indoor units for a Russley home

Not every room suits a high wall unit. Say your lounge has a picture window with little wall above it. A [floor console](/single-room-heat-pump-installation/) can sit under the sill. In a bedroom, a small, quiet wall unit is usually best. With good ceiling space, [ducted heating](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/) can serve every room with nothing on the walls.

We size each indoor unit from the room it heats. We look at the insulation above it and the glass in it. Then we match the outdoor unit, or units, to that.

## Heat pumps exposed to Russley's western weather

Out on the plains, Russley gets hard frosts on clear nights. In spring it gets nor'west gales. On icy mornings a heat pump has to defrost its outdoor coil. It does that better up off the ground, with the water draining freely away. On the windy side, a unit can lose airflow. So shelter is part of how we choose the spot. If your house is very exposed, our [cold climate heat pumps](/cold-climate-heat-pumps/) guide explains which features help.

## Heating a Russley formal lounge you rarely use

Some Russley homes have a second, formal lounge that sits empty most evenings. Heating it every day wastes power. Leaving it cold lets damp settle in. A small unit you switch on when guests come solves it. So does a ducted zone you can close off. Either way you keep the option, without paying to heat an empty room.

## Neighbours and outdoor unit noise in Russley

Russley comes under the Christchurch District Plan. It judges outdoor unit noise at the neighbouring site. It doesn't set a distance from the fence. Our [boundary rules guide](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/) gives the night limit and how to place a unit to stay under it.
