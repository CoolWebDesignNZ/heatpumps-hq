## Heat pumps for Bromley's mid-century homes

Most Bromley homes are single storey houses on piles. They're weatherboard or brick. Many are state or private houses from the 1950s to 70s, on modest sections. The first thing that decides the heat pump is how these houses lose heat.

- **Floors on piles.** Cold air moves under the house. With no underfloor insulation, the floor pulls heat out of every room. We check under the house before we size anything.
- **Weatherboard walls.** Houses this age may have little or no wall insulation, unless someone's added it. A lounge in one needs a bigger unit than the same room in a newer home.
- **A front lounge with bedrooms off a hall.** A lounge unit heats the lounge and not much else. More on that below.

Bromley is an older east side suburb. Its streets run right beside commercial and industrial land. That includes the city's wastewater treatment plant towards the estuary. So there are two more things to plan for. Dust, and close neighbours.

**Dust.** Homes near trucks, busy roads and yards get more fine dust. A heat pump pulls room air through its filters all day. So the filters fill up faster. Blocked filters cut the heat, work the fan harder and can cause smells. We put the indoor unit where you can reach it. You need to lift the front panel and pull the filters out.

**Neighbours close by.** Modest sections put houses near each other. Christchurch measures heat pump noise at the neighbour's boundary. The night limit is the one that counts on cold mornings. We put the outdoor unit away from the people next door. We keep it out of corners that bounce sound. And we sit it on anti-vibration feet.

## Heat pump installation in a mid-century Bromley house

It starts with the layout. Usually there's a front lounge, with the kitchen and dining behind it and bedrooms off a hall. Some of these houses have been fully done up. Others still have little insulation in the walls. We check the ceiling, the underfloor and the windows. We measure the rooms. Then we size the unit from what's really there.

For a warm lounge, one well placed wall unit is often enough. If you want the bedrooms warm too, look at a second unit or a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/).

## Outdoor units on Bromley sections

Bromley sections are flat. That keeps the outdoor unit simple. We use a level pad or a wall bracket. It's lifted so it drains, and kept clear of bins and fences. Is the unit near a dusty driveway or yard? Hose down the coil now and then so it can breathe.
