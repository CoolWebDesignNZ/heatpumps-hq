## Is it time for a heat pump replacement?

A heat pump that's been running for many years doesn't automatically need replacing. These are the signs that it might:

- **It can't heat the room on frosty mornings,** even after a service and clean filters.
- **The same fault keeps coming back,** or a major part such as the compressor has failed.
- **It uses an older refrigerant** that is becoming hard and costly to get for repairs.
- **The power bill has crept up** with no change in how you use it.
- **The house has changed.** A renovation, an extension or new insulation means the room needs a different size of unit.

If only one of these applies, a repair or service may be enough. [Heat pump repair](/heat-pump-repair/) explains how we weigh up repairing against replacing.

## Heat pump upgrades short of replacement

Not every efficiency problem needs a new heat pump. Before we quote for a replacement, we look at whether one of these would fix it:

- **A full service.** A dirty indoor coil and fan, a blocked outdoor coil or a slow refrigerant leak all cut output. A [service](/heat-pump-servicing/) puts a sound unit back to its best.
- **Moving the outdoor unit.** A unit boxed in by a fence or tight cover recirculates its own cold air. Moving it into open air can make a real difference.
- **Better controls.** A [Wi-Fi controller](/smart-heat-pump-systems/) lets you run timers and schedules properly instead of leaving the unit on all day or switching it on high when the house is cold.
- **Adding a unit.** If one heat pump is trying to heat a whole open plan house, a second smaller unit in the far end may suit better than one bigger replacement. See [multi-room installation](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/).

## How we replace an old heat pump

Replacing an old heat pump runs in six steps, from looking at the house to showing you the new controls:

1. **We look at the old system and the house.** The rooms, the insulation, the windows and how you use the space. The old unit's size is a starting point, not the answer, because the house or how you use it may have changed since it went in.
2. **You get options with a fixed price.** Usually a like-for-like position and, where it helps, a better one. For each model we show you the Cold zone heating stars and heating capacity at 2°C from the energy label, so you can compare running efficiency.
3. **We remove the old unit.** The refrigerant is recovered before the old unit leaves your property.
4. **We install the new system.** Brackets or feet, pipework and drainage, all tidy. Existing pipework is only reused when it suits the new unit and is in good condition; otherwise it's replaced.
5. **The electrical connection is done by a licensed electrical worker.** WorkSafe says electricians must issue a Certificate of Compliance for fixed wiring work, and you should keep it with your house records ([WorkSafe, getting electrical work done](https://www.worksafe.govt.nz/managing-health-and-safety/consumers/safe-living-with-electricity/getting-electrical-work-done)).
6. **We commission and test it** in heating and cooling, then show you how to use the controls.

## Comparing old and new heat pump efficiency

The energy label changed in 2021. EECA says the stars on the old Energy Rating Label measured something different and can't be compared with the stars on the current Zoned Energy Rating Label ([EECA guide](https://www.eeca.govt.nz/assets/EECA-Resources/Product-regulations/The-new-Zoned-Energy-Rating-Label-for-heat-pumps-air-conditioners.pdf)). So we don't promise a percentage saving. What we can tell you is how the new unit's rated output compares with what your room needs, and how it rates against other models of the same size. For each part of the label, see [reading the energy rating label](/energy-star-heat-pumps/).
