## Why fault finding comes first

A heat pump has a few separate systems working together: the power supply, the controls, the fans and airflow, the refrigerant circuit, the sensors and the communication link between the indoor and outdoor units. A symptom like "not heating" can start in any of them.

Swapping parts until the problem goes away is slow and expensive. We test each system in turn and only quote a repair once we know which part has failed and why.

If you'd like to rule out the simple things yourself first, our [DIY troubleshooting guide](/heat-pump-problems-troubleshooting-guide/) covers the checks that don't need tools.

## What we test when your heat pump is not working

1. **Power and wiring.** Supply voltage at the outdoor unit, the isolating switch, the breaker and the connections between the two units.
2. **Controls and settings.** Mode, timers, the remote or wall controller, and any settings that limit output.
3. **Airflow.** Filters, the indoor coil, both fans and anything restricting air around the outdoor unit.
4. **The refrigerant circuit.** Pressures and pipe temperatures in heating and cooling, which show whether the charge is right and the compressor and valves are doing their job.
5. **Sensors.** The temperature sensors on the coils, pipes and air intake, tested against the manufacturer's values.
6. **Fault history.** Any codes the unit has stored, read with the manufacturer's service information for that model.

## Heat pump blowing cold air

What you see tells us where to start looking:

- **Cold air for a few minutes, then warm.** On a frosty morning the outdoor unit melts its ice by running in reverse for a short time. That's normal.
- **Cold air all the time in heat mode.** Something is stopping the refrigerant circuit from heating. Low refrigerant, an outdoor fan that isn't spinning, a compressor that won't start, or a stuck reversing valve are the usual suspects.
- **Lukewarm air that never gets hot.** Often blocked filters or a dirty coil, sometimes a sensor reading the wrong temperature. Our [thermostat and sensor checks](/heat-pump-thermostat-calibration/) page covers that case.

## Heat pump flashing lights and error codes

When a heat pump detects a fault it usually stops, flashes its operation or timer light in a set pattern, or shows a code on the remote or wall controller. Every brand has its own system, and the same code can mean different things on different models.

We don't guess at code meanings. If your unit is flashing:

- Count the flashes or film them on your phone.
- Note the brand and the model number from the sticker on the indoor unit.
- Write down what the unit was doing when it stopped.

That information often lets us bring the right part on the first visit. Your model's manual on the manufacturer's New Zealand website will list its codes.

## Heat pump not turning on at all

A unit that shows no sign of life is often a power problem rather than a heat pump problem. We check the breaker, the isolating switch, the remote and the indoor unit's control board in that order. A unit that tries to start and then stops is different, and usually points to a protection device shutting it down for a reason we need to find.

## Runs but the house stays cold

If the unit is running and blowing warm air but the room never gets comfortable, the heat pump may be undersized for the space, losing heat to open doors or a cold room next door, or losing output through a dirty outdoor coil. We'll tell you whether it's a fault or a sizing issue. Sizing is covered in our [heat pump sizing guide](/heat-pump-sizing/).
