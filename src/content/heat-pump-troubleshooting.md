## Why fault finding comes first

"Not heating" can start in any part of a heat pump. The power supply. The controls. The fans and airflow. The refrigerant. The sensors. Or the link between the indoor and outdoor units.

Swapping parts till the problem goes away is slow and costly. We test each part of the system in turn. We only quote a repair once we know what failed and why.

Want to rule out the simple things first? Try these [DIY troubleshooting checks](/heat-pump-problems-troubleshooting-guide/). You don't need tools.

## What we test when your heat pump is not working

We test in this order.

1. **Power and wiring.** Voltage at the outdoor unit. The isolating switch and the breaker. The connections between the two units.
2. **Controls and settings.** Mode and timers. The remote or wall controller. Any settings that hold back output.
3. **Airflow.** Filters, the indoor coil and both fans. Anything blocking air round the outdoor unit.
4. **Refrigerant.** Pressures and pipe temperatures in heating and cooling. They show if the gas level is right. They also show if the compressor and valves are working.
5. **Sensors.** The temperature sensors on the coils, pipes and air intake. We test them against the maker's values.
6. **Fault history.** Any codes it has saved. We read them with the maker's service info for that model.

## Heat pump blowing cold air

Sometimes that's normal. Sometimes it's a fault. What you're seeing tells us where to start.

- **Cold air for a few minutes, then warm.** On a frosty morning the outdoor unit runs backwards for a bit to melt its ice. That's normal.
- **Cold air all the time on heat.** Something's stopping the refrigerant side from heating. Usual suspects are low gas, an outdoor fan that won't spin, a compressor that won't start, or a stuck reversing valve.
- **Lukewarm air that never gets hot.** Often blocked filters or a dirty coil. Sometimes a sensor reading the wrong temperature. We test that with [thermostat and sensor calibration](/heat-pump-thermostat-calibration/).

## Heat pump flashing lights and error codes

When a heat pump finds a fault, it usually stops. It flashes its operation or timer light in a set pattern. Or it shows a code on the remote or wall controller. Every brand does it differently. The same code can mean different things on different models.

We don't guess what codes mean. If yours is flashing:

- Count the flashes, or film them on your phone.
- Note the brand and the model number from the sticker on the indoor unit.
- Write down what it was doing when it stopped.

With that, we can often bring the right part on the first visit. Your model's manual on the maker's New Zealand website lists its codes.

## Heat pump not turning on at all

No sign of life? It's often a power problem, not a heat pump problem. We check the breaker, then the isolating switch, the remote and the indoor control board. A unit that tries to start, then stops, is different. That usually means a safety device is shutting it down. We need to find out why.

## Runs but the house stays cold

It's running and blowing warm air, but the room never gets comfy? The heat pump may be too small for the space. Heat may be escaping through open doors or into a cold room next door. Or a dirty outdoor coil may be cutting its output. We'll tell you if it's a fault or a [sizing](/heat-pump-sizing/) problem.
