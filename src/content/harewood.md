## Harewood homes

Harewood sits on Christchurch's north-west fringe, beside the airport. Part of it is suburban, with family homes on standard sections, and part is semi-rural, with larger properties, lifestyle blocks and open paddocks. The land is flat and open, which shapes both its winters and its summers.

## Winter: open ground, hard frost

Flat, open land with little shelter cools quickly on clear nights. Harewood's outer properties in particular can wake up to heavy frost. For the outdoor unit, that means:

- raised feet or a wall bracket, so it sits above ice and pooled water
- a clear drainage path for defrost water
- shelter from the coldest wind without boxing the unit in

We size the system so it holds the house at a comfortable temperature on those mornings, using the room sizes, windows and insulation of your home.

## Summer: nor'west heat

Canterbury's hot, dry nor'west wind can make summer days uncomfortable, especially in rooms facing north and west. A heat pump [cools as well as heats](/air-conditioning-installation-christchurch/), so the same unit that warms your lounge in July can keep it bearable in January. When we place the indoor unit, we think about both: where warm air needs to go in winter and where cool air helps most in summer.

## Suburban and semi-rural

In Harewood's suburban streets the work is familiar: a lounge unit, bedroom heating added as needed, and outdoor units placed thoughtfully near boundaries. On larger properties there's more room for the outdoor unit, but pipe runs to a bigger house can be longer, so we plan the route to keep it efficient.

## Filters in a dusty summer

Dry nor'west days carry dust, and on semi-rural properties so do paddocks, gravel driveways and cut hay. A unit that runs hard on cooling through summer can clog its filters as fast as it does in winter. Rinse them every few weeks while the unit is in heavy use, and book a yearly service to clean the coils and check the drain.
