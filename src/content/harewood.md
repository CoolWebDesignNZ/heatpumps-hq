## Heat pump installation in Harewood homes

A Harewood heat pump has two jobs. It has to keep the house warm through hard frosts. And it has to cool it on hot nor'west days. Harewood sits on the north-west edge of Christchurch, beside the airport. The land is flat and open, with little shelter, so you get both extremes. That makes the cooling side of a split system as useful as the heating side. It also means we size for the coldest mornings, not an average day.

- **Suburban family homes on standard sections.** Most have a lounge and a hall of bedrooms. A high wall unit sized to the lounge does the main work. A small unit in each bedroom that stays cold covers the rest. North and west facing rooms are the ones you'll want to cool in summer, so they get first call on a unit.
- **Semi-rural homes, lifestyle blocks and houses next to open paddocks.** These tend to be bigger, with more living space and longer walks between rooms. They often suit a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) or ducted heating. The outdoor unit has to stand up to frost, wind and dust.

At the quote we measure the rooms and check which way the windows face. We look at the insulation. Then we pick an outdoor spot with shelter, airflow and drainage.

## Harewood winters: open ground, hard frost

Flat, open land cools fast on clear nights. The outer Harewood properties in particular can wake up to a heavy frost. For the outdoor unit, that means:

- **Raised feet or a wall bracket**, so it sits above ice and pooled water.
- **A clear path** for the defrost water to drain.
- **Shelter from the coldest wind**, without boxing the unit in.

We size the system to hold your house at a comfortable temperature on those mornings. We work from your room sizes, windows and insulation.

## Heat pumps for Harewood's nor'west summers

Canterbury's hot, dry nor'west can make summer days hard going. Rooms facing north and west get it worst. A heat pump [cools as well as heats](/air-conditioning-installation-christchurch/). The unit that warms your lounge in July can keep it bearable in January. So when we place the indoor unit, we think about both seasons. Where does warm air need to go in winter? Where does cool air help most in summer?

## Suburban and semi-rural Harewood

On Harewood's suburban streets, the house next door is close. We keep the outdoor unit away from your neighbour's bedroom windows. We also keep it off the boundary fence, where sound would bounce back. Bigger properties have more room for the outdoor unit. But the pipe run to a large house can be longer, and a long run loses some output. So we put the outdoor unit as close as we can to the rooms it heats.

## Heat pump service in a dusty Harewood summer

Dry nor'west days carry dust. On semi-rural blocks, so do paddocks, gravel driveways and cut hay. If your unit runs hard on cooling all summer, the filters can clog as fast as they do in winter. Rinse them every few weeks while it's working hard. Book a yearly service too, so we can clean the coils and check the drain.
