## What gets dirty inside a heat pump, and where

Air comes in the top of the indoor unit. It goes through the filters and over the coil. Then a long barrel fan pushes it out. Each part picks up something different.

- **Filters** catch hair, lint and coarse dust. They're the bit you see.
- **The coil** catches fine dust that gets past the filters. It's wet in cooling and dry mode. Wet dust grows mould.
- **The fan wheel** gets a sticky grey or black coating on each blade. It flicks bits of it out of the unit. That's where the specks on the louvres and wall come from.
- **The drain tray** under the coil holds a film of slime. It can block the drain.

Cleaning the filters only deals with the first one.

## How to clean your heat pump filters

It takes a few minutes.

1. Turn it off with the remote. Then at the isolator too, if you can reach it.
2. Lift the front panel until it clicks open.
3. Slide the filters down and out.
4. Vacuum off the dust. Then rinse them from the back under a lukewarm tap. A drop of mild dishwashing liquid helps with grease.
5. Shake off the water. Let them dry in the shade. Hot sun can warp them.
6. Slide them back in the right way round. Close the panel.

Do it every few weeks while you use it every day. Some units have extra filters clipped behind the main ones. Your manual says if those wash or need replacing.

## When to book heat pump cleaning in Christchurch

Book a clean if you notice any of these.

- It smells when it starts. A dirt smell isn't the same as a fault smell. Learn to tell [heat pump smells](/heat-pump-deodorising/) apart.
- You can see black spots inside the outlet when the louvres open.
- There are grey marks on the wall or ceiling above it.
- Someone at home has allergies or asthma, and it hasn't been cleaned for a while.
- You've moved in and don't know when it was last cleaned.
- It's been cooling through a long summer.
