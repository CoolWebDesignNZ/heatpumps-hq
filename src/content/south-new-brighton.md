## Heat pump installation on both sides of South New Brighton

In South New Brighton, which side you live on sets most of the plan. The suburb is a narrow stretch of land. The beach is on one side. The Avon Heathcote Estuary is on the other.

- **Beach-side homes.** They take sea spray and the full easterly. The outdoor unit goes on the sheltered side of the house, out of the sea wind. Its coil needs rinsing often to keep the salt off.
- **Estuary-side homes.** There's less spray here. But the air off the water is often damp. Low-lying sections can stay wet all winter. The outdoor unit sits on a raised base or wall bracket, clear of the wet ground. Indoor units go where they keep the bedrooms warm enough to stop condensation. More on that below.

## Damp homes near the estuary

A cold house near the estuary often shows it in the windows. You get condensation in the morning and mould in the corners of bedrooms. The best thing a heat pump does here is keep rooms at a steady temperature. Warm surfaces don't collect as much moisture. A low, constant setting through winter usually works better than an hour of hard heating in the evening.

If damp is a problem, we look at the bedrooms too. A small unit in the main bedroom stops the back of the house turning cold and wet overnight. So does a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/).

## Soft ground and outdoor units in South New Brighton

Much of South New Brighton is sand. Some areas settled after the earthquakes. A slab dropped onto loose ground can sink at one end. That tips the unit, and it can't drain its defrost water. So we either build a firm, level base or mount the unit on a wall bracket. Then it doesn't rely on the ground at all. Either way, it sits high enough to stay out of puddles.
