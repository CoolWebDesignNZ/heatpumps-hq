## When a heat pump replacement beats a repair

A heat pump can be repaired for years, and often should be. But there comes a point where the money goes into an old unit that won't last. These are the signs we look for.

- **Compressor failure on an old unit.** The compressor is the most expensive part to replace. On an older system, the cost of the part and labour can approach the price of a new unit, and the rest of the system is just as old.
- **Parts no longer made.** Once a model is out of production, parts get scarce and dear. A repair can stall waiting for a part that may never arrive.
- **Old refrigerant.** Older units run on refrigerants that are being phased out. Recharging an old system can be hard and costly, and the leak that caused the loss usually comes back.
- **Repeated faults.** If the same fault keeps returning, or a new one follows each repair, the unit is telling you it's near the end.
- **Poor efficiency.** An old unit that runs constantly and still feels weak is costing you every month on power.

If the fault is minor and the unit is young, a [heat pump repair](/heat-pump-repair/) is the right call. If you're not sure, an [emergency repair visit](/emergency-heat-pump-repair/) can tell you what's failed and what a fix would cost.

## What a replacement heat pump involves

A replacement is more than swapping one box for another. The old system has to come out properly before the new one goes in.

- **Refrigerant recovery.** The gas in the old system is pumped out and collected. It isn't vented to the air. This happens before any pipe is disconnected.
- **Removing both units.** The indoor unit comes off its bracket and the outdoor unit off its feet or pad. The old pipework and cable go with them.
- **Checking the old route.** We look at the pipe route, the wall hole, the drain and the electrical circuit. Some of it can be reused. Some of it has to change.
- **Sizing the new unit.** We work out the heat the room needs, not the kW of the old unit.
- **Installing and commissioning.** The new system is pressure tested, vacuumed and run in heating and cooling before we hand it over. The full job is set out under [heat pump installation in Christchurch](/heat-pump-installation-in-christchurch/).

## What a heat pump replacement can reuse

Reusing the old pipe route and hole keeps the job tidy and the cost down. But it only works if the parts suit the new system.

- **The pipe route.** The old pipes must be the right diameter for the new model, in good condition and pressure tight. If they aren't, we run new pipe, sometimes along the same path.
- **The wall hole.** Often it can be reused. A different indoor unit may sit at a different height, so the hole may need to move or be patched.
- **The drain.** The old drain can often stay if it falls away properly and isn't blocked. A new indoor unit may need a different drain route.
- **The electrical circuit.** The circuit has to suit what the new unit draws. If the switchboard has no spare capacity or the cable is too small, a licensed electrical worker sorts it before the unit goes in.
- **The outdoor spot.** The old pad or bracket may take the new unit. If the old unit was badly placed, we'll suggest a better spot.

We check all of this before you agree to anything, so there are no surprises on the day.

## Heat pump replacement sizing, not matching the old unit

The most common mistake with a replacement is ordering the same kW as the old unit. Old units were often the wrong size to begin with.

- **Oversized units.** Too big a unit heats quickly, then cycles on and off. That's less comfortable, harder on the compressor and can cost more to run.
- **Undersized units.** Too small a unit runs flat out on cold nights and never quite gets there. It uses more power for less heat.
- **Rooms change.** Insulation, glazing, extensions and how a room is used can all change what it needs. A unit that was right twenty years ago may not be right now.

We size the replacement on the room itself: its size, insulation, glazing and how you use it. The method is set out under [heat pump sizing](/heat-pump-sizing/), and the [single room calculator](/calculator-single-room-heat-pump/) gives a rough starting point.

## Choosing a heat pump replacement unit

You don't have to stay with the same brand or type. A replacement is a chance to get the right unit for the room.

- **High wall units** suit most living areas and bedrooms. See [high wall heat pumps](/high-wall-heat-pumps/).
- **Floor consoles** work where there's no good high wall space, or under a window. See [floor mounted heat pumps](/floor-mounted-heat-pumps/).
- **Multi room systems** replace an old single unit with several indoor units on one outdoor unit. See [multi room heat pumps](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/).
