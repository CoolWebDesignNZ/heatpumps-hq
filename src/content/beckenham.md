## Heat pumps for Beckenham villas and bungalows

Most Beckenham homes are timber villas and bungalows. Many sit on well established sections with big trees. Two things decide the heat pump. How these older houses lose heat, and where they stay damp.

- **Villas:** high ceilings, and separate rooms off a central hall. Often there's single glazing and not much under the floor. Warm air rises and sits up by the ceiling. So the unit needs enough fan power to push it back down to you. Every room you use needs its own unit, or one on a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/).
- **Bungalows:** lower ceilings, so a high wall unit heats the lounge well. The bedrooms still need their own heat. A lounge unit won't carry warmth round the hall.
- **Renovations and rebuilds:** these are usually better insulated and more open plan. One unit, sized right, can often heat the whole living area.
- **By the river or under big trees:** these houses get less winter sun. Beckenham sits on the flat between the city and the foot of the Port Hills. On this low ground near the river, those homes run colder and damper. We size for that and set the system up for steady heat. More below.

Before we size anything, we check the ceiling and underfloor insulation. In an older timber house, that's where most of the heat goes.

## Damp, shade and cold air in Beckenham

Low ground by a river, plus big old trees, can make a house feel colder and damper than one a few streets away. Rooms on the shady side get little winter sun. Cold surfaces gather condensation. A heat pump helps in three ways:

- **Steady heat:** walls and windows stay warmer, so less moisture settles on them.
- **Dry mode:** pulls moisture out on mild, damp days without making the room too hot.
- **Timers:** warm the house before you get up. That's often when condensation is worst.

Run it at a steady, middling setting, not in short bursts. That lets the walls warm right through. It doesn't replace ventilation, though. Rangehoods and bathroom fans vented outside still do the main work on moisture. So does airing the house often.

## Heat pump installation in an older Beckenham home

Inside a villa or bungalow, we look for a wall where the indoor unit can push air into the room. It shouldn't sit on a picture rail, a fireplace surround or fancy mouldings. In a high-stud room, a [floor console](/single-room-heat-pump-installation/) can be the neater choice.

Outside, the unit goes where it can breathe and drain. Under a big tree is fine, as long as the leaves are kept clear. On a frosty morning the unit defrosts and lets off meltwater. So we raise it on feet or a bracket, clear of soggy ground and garden beds.
