## Beckenham character homes by the river

Beckenham sits on the flat between the city and the foot of the Port Hills, with the Heathcote River winding through it. It's known for its timber villas and bungalows, many on well established sections with big trees. Renovations and rebuilds sit among the older homes, but the overall feel is character housing. Some streets back onto the river itself, and others are a short walk from it.

Those features shape a heat pump install here more than the size of the house does.

## Damp, shade and cold air in Beckenham

Low ground near a river, combined with mature trees, can make a house feel colder and damper than its neighbours a few streets away. Rooms on the shaded side get little winter sun, and cold surfaces collect condensation. A heat pump helps in three ways:

- **Steady heat** keeps walls and windows warmer, so less moisture condenses on them.
- **Dry mode** removes moisture on mild, damp days without overheating the room.
- **Timers** warm the house before you get up, which is when condensation is often worst.

Run it at a steady, moderate setting rather than in short bursts, which lets wall surfaces warm through. It isn't a replacement for ventilation. Rangehoods and bathroom fans vented outside, plus regular airing, still do the main work on moisture.

## Heat pump installation in an older Beckenham home

Inside a villa or bungalow, we look for a wall that lets the indoor unit push air into the room without sitting on top of a picture rail, a fireplace surround or ornate mouldings. In a high-stud room, a [floor console](/single-room-heat-pump-installation/) can be the neater choice.

Outside, the unit goes where it can breathe and drain. Under a big tree is acceptable if leaves are kept clear. On a frosty morning the unit runs a defrost cycle and lets off meltwater, so we raise it on feet or a bracket clear of soggy ground and garden beds.
