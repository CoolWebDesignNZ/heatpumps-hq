## What is a heat pump?

The heat pump in most Christchurch homes is an **air-to-air, reverse-cycle split system**. There's an outdoor unit with a fan, a coil and a compressor, and an indoor unit with its own coil and fan. Two insulated copper pipes join them, carrying a refrigerant that circulates in a sealed loop.

EECA describes it in plain terms: the system uses a relatively small amount of electricity to extract heat from one place, usually the outdoor air, and deliver it somewhere else. Run the other way, the same equipment cools the house. Source: [EECA, Good practice guide: heat pump installation](https://www.eeca.govt.nz/assets/EECA-Resources/Good-Practice-Guide-Heat-Pump-Installation-March-2022.pdf), sections 1 and 2.

A heat pump is a fridge pointed the other way. A fridge pulls heat out of the food compartment and dumps it into your kitchen through the coil at the back. A heat pump pulls heat out of the garden and dumps it into your lounge.

## How does a heat pump work, step by step

In heating mode the refrigerant goes through five stages, over and over:

1. **Outdoor coil (evaporator).** Cold, low-pressure liquid refrigerant flows through the outdoor coil. The fan pulls outside air across it. The refrigerant is colder than that air, so it soaks up heat and boils into a gas.
2. **Compressor.** The compressor squeezes that gas into a much smaller volume. Squeezing a gas makes it hot, so the refrigerant leaves the compressor as a hot, high-pressure gas.
3. **Indoor coil (condenser).** The hot gas travels through the pipes to the indoor unit. The indoor fan blows room air across the coil, the refrigerant gives up its heat and turns back into a liquid, and warm air comes out of the unit.
4. **Expansion (metering) device.** The liquid passes through a restriction that drops its pressure suddenly, which also drops its temperature well below the outdoor air.
5. **Back outside.** The cold liquid returns to the outdoor coil, ready to pick up more heat.

For cooling, a **reversing valve** swaps the direction of flow. The indoor coil becomes the cold side and takes heat out of the room, and the outdoor coil gets rid of it outside.

## Why it uses less electricity than a heater

An electric fan heater or oil column heater turns 1 unit of electricity into 1 unit of heat. A heat pump uses its electricity to run the compressor and fans, and the heat it delivers is that energy plus everything it collected from the outdoor air.

EECA's guide illustrates it like this: if 1 kWh of electricity drives the heat pump and 2 kWh is drawn from the outside air, up to 3 kWh of heat arrives indoors. That ratio is the **coefficient of performance (COP)**, heat out divided by electricity in. The guide treats a COP of at least 3 as acceptable, with better products reaching 4 or more.

Every heat pump sold in New Zealand carries an energy rating label, so you can compare models and pick an [energy efficient heat pump](/energy-efficient/) that costs less to run.

## How inverter compressors change the cycle

Older fixed-speed heat pumps switched fully on, then fully off. Modern units commonly use a **variable-speed (inverter) compressor**, and EECA notes that fixed-speed units are no longer common. It starts gently, runs hard while the room warms up, then slows down as the room gets close to the set temperature and ticks over to hold it.

According to EECA, that gives a steadier room temperature, better efficiency, less vibration and quieter running. It's also why a correctly sized heat pump should spend most of its time running steadily at part speed rather than cycling on and off.

## How heat pumps work when it's cold outside

A heat pump's output and efficiency are not fixed numbers. They fall as the gap between the outdoor air and the room widens. The rated heating capacity on the label is measured at 7°C outside, and on a frosty Christchurch morning the unit is working against much colder air than that.

Two things happen in the cold:

- **Less heat out.** The colder the air, the less heat the outdoor coil can collect. Some models keep working well below freezing and others struggle, which is why the label also shows heating output at 2°C. That difference is what sets [cold climate heat pumps](/cold-climate-heat-pumps/) apart.
- **Defrost cycles.** Below about 7°C, moisture in the air can freeze onto the outdoor coil. The unit reverses for a few minutes to melt it, and no heat is delivered while it does. You may see steam rising from the outdoor unit. EECA notes that an undersized heat pump has to defrost more often.

This is the main reason sizing matters so much in Canterbury. EECA's guide lists the external design temperature for Christchurch City and Selwyn District as **minus 4°C**, and when [sizing a heat pump](/heat-pump-sizing/) a unit should be chosen for its output at that temperature, not at 7°C.

## What the indoor and outdoor units actually do

The outdoor unit collects heat and the compressor in it raises the refrigerant's pressure and temperature, while the indoor unit releases that heat into the room:

| Part | Where | Job in heating mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outdoor coil and fan | Outside | Collects heat from the outdoor air |
| Compressor | Outdoor unit | Raises the refrigerant's pressure and temperature |
| Indoor coil and fan | In the room | Releases heat into the room air |
| Filters | Behind the indoor unit's front panel | Catch dust before it reaches the coil |
| Condensate drain | From the indoor unit, and outside | Carries away water from cooling and defrost |

The indoor unit can be a high wall unit, a floor console, a ceiling cassette or a ducted system feeding vents in several rooms. These [types of heat pumps](/types-of-heat-pumps/) all work on the same cycle.

## Keeping a heat pump working well

The cycle only works if air can move freely across both coils. Clogged filters, a dusty indoor coil or leaves packed around the outdoor unit all cut output and push up power use. EECA suggests cleaning the filters as often as monthly when the unit is in regular use, and a yearly [service](/maintenance-servicing/) keeps the coils, fans and drains clean.
