## Newer homes on the slopes

Westmorland is a hill suburb on the lower Port Hills, between Cashmere and Hoon Hay. Compared with the older suburbs around it, the housing is newer, the homes are generally larger, and many are built over two levels to follow the slope. Insulation and glazing in newer homes are better than in older housing, which changes the sizing.

A well insulated large house doesn't need huge capacity. It needs heat delivered to the right places.

## Zoning a larger house

Big homes have rooms that are used at very different times: a family room and kitchen in the evening, a study during the day, bedrooms at night, a guest room rarely. Heating every room to one temperature uses more power than the household needs. [Zoning](/heat-pump-zoning/) splits it up.

Three ways we do it:

- **Separate wall units** in the rooms that need them, each with its own controller.
- **A multi-room system**, with several indoor units on one outdoor unit, which keeps the outside of the house tidier.
- **Ducted with zones**, where dampers send air only to the parts of the house you select.

On a two-level home, upstairs and downstairs often need different settings, because warm air rises up the stairwell. Separate zones handle that.

## Sun and glass on the hill

On the slope, a lot of homes look out over the city through large living room windows. Those rooms collect strong winter sun during the day and lose heat through the glass at night. They can also overheat in summer, which is where the cooling side of a heat pump earns its place. We size the living area unit for both.

## Outdoor units for hillside sections

Newer sections on the hill are often terraced, with retaining walls and a garage below the living level. Common positions for the outdoor unit are beside the garage, on a bracket on a solid wall, or on a pad on a lower terrace. Each needs clear airflow, a drain path and service access. The [boundary guide](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/) covers noise toward neighbours, which travels on slopes.
