## Heat pumps for Northwood homes

Northwood sits in the north of Christchurch, on flat ground. Most of the housing went up from the 1990s onward, so the streets are lined with brick and plaster homes on standard sections, along with townhouses and smaller units. The houses are generally well insulated and double glazed.

That changes what a heat pump has to do. A newer, tight house holds its heat, so a unit that is too big will heat the room fast and then switch off, which wastes power and feels uneven. Getting the size right matters more here than adding capacity.

- **Standalone family homes.** Usually one high wall unit in the living area and sometimes a second in the main bedroom. The house holds heat well, so the unit can be smaller than the floor area alone would suggest.
- **Townhouses and units.** Often a single unit in the living space, with the outdoor unit on a bracket or in a small side yard. Placement is the main job, because neighbouring units sit close.
- **Open plan living.** These need a unit sized for the whole space, not just the wall it hangs on. We work that out at the quote.

## Northwood's climate and what it means for your heat pump

Northwood is on the flat, inland from the coast, so it gets cold winter mornings with frost. It does not get the salt air of the beach suburbs, so the outdoor unit is not fighting corrosion the way a coastal one is. What it does face is cold starts on frosty mornings, when the unit works hardest to bring the house up to temperature.

A cold climate model handles those mornings better. If you are choosing a unit, look at [cold climate heat pumps](/cold-climate-heat-pumps/). Setting a steady temperature rather than big swings also helps, and [zoning](/heat-pump-zoning/) can keep unused rooms out of the picture.
