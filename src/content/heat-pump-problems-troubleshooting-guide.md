## Four checks for any heat pump problem

Whatever the symptom, try these four things before you call anyone:

1. **The remote.** Put in fresh batteries. Check the mode and the set temperature. Make sure no timer or weekly schedule is turning it off.
2. **The filters.** Open the front panel and slide them out. Vacuum or rinse them. Let them dry before they go back in.
3. **The outdoor unit.** Keep the back, sides and front clear. No leaves or snow packed against it. No solid ice.
4. **A power reset.** Turn it off at the isolating switch beside the outdoor unit. Wait five minutes, then turn it back on.

**Stay safe.** Don't take panels or covers off either unit. Don't poke anything into the fans. Don't hose the outdoor unit while it's running.

## Common heat pump problems, symptom by symptom

Find your symptom below. Then work through the checks under it.

### Not heating, or blowing cool air

- **Mode:** is it on heat? Auto and dry can both blow cool air.
- **Set temperature:** is it higher than the room?
- **Defrost:** on a cold morning the indoor fan stops and the outdoor unit steams for a few minutes. Give it time.
- **Filters:** are they clean?
- **Outdoor unit:** is it clear, and is the fan turning?

Still blowing cool air for long stretches? Then the problem is inside the system. It could be low gas, a sensor or a valve. That calls for [fault finding](/heat-pump-troubleshooting/). For more on cold weather, read [how heat pumps cope below zero](/low-temperature-heat-pumps/).

### Won't turn on

- **Remote:** check the batteries. Stand closer and point it straight at the indoor unit.
- **Power:** check the breaker on the switchboard and the isolating switch outside.
- **Lights:** look for a flashing light on the indoor unit. That's the unit telling you it has a fault.

If the breaker trips again straight after you reset it, that's an electrical fault. Leave it off and call.

### Flashing light or error code

Write down the code or the flash pattern. Then try one power reset. Codes change from brand to brand and model to model. Look yours up in the manual for your model. If the code comes back, ring us with it. It shows a technician where to start.

### Water leaking from the indoor unit

It's usually the condensate drain. It may be blocked, kinked or not sloped enough. A very dirty coil can do it too. Turn the unit off so no more water builds up, and put a towel underneath. You'll need a technician for this. Leave it and it can stain your walls and ceilings.

### Ice on the outdoor unit that won't clear

A light frost that comes and goes is normal. A solid block of ice isn't, and nor is ice building up in the base. Clear the path under the unit so the meltwater can drain away. Leave the ice on the coil alone, though. If it keeps building up, the defrost or gas side needs a check.

### Bad smell

A musty smell when it starts means mould and dust on the coil and fan. Clean the filters first. If the smell stays, the coil needs a proper clean.

A burning or electrical smell is a different matter. Turn it off at the isolating switch and call. We cover other smells, and how we get rid of them, under [heat pump deodorising](/heat-pump-deodorising/).

### Noisy

Rattles, buzzing and grinding usually come from loose panels, vibration or worn fan bearings. Every sound has its own cause and fix. We go through them in [heat pump noise problems and solutions](/heat-pump-noise-problems-solutions/).

### Room never gets to temperature

- **Doors:** are they open to the rest of the house?
- **Curtains:** are they open after dark?
- **Fan:** is it on low?
- **Size:** is the unit too small for the room?

Does the room get close but never quite there? The temperature sensor may be reading wrong. Read about [thermostat calibration](/heat-pump-thermostat-calibration/). Does it fall well short only on cold mornings? The unit may be too small. [Heat pump sizing](/heat-pump-sizing/) explains why that shows up in the cold.

### Turning on and off often

Short bursts of running and stopping have three usual causes. The set temperature may be too close to the room temperature. The unit may be too big for the room. Or it's sensing warm air near itself and stopping early. Try a setting a little higher or lower, with the fan on auto. If it keeps happening, get it checked.

### Power bill has jumped

Check the timer, the mode and the set temperature. Then clean the filters. If nothing obvious has changed, look at the other causes of high [heat pump power use](/energy-consumption-monitoring/).

## When to stop troubleshooting and call a technician

Turn the heat pump off at the isolating switch and book a visit if you have any of these:

- **Burning:** a burning smell, smoke, sparks or loud electrical buzzing
- **Leaks:** water coming out inside
- **Tripping:** a breaker that keeps going
- **Ice:** ice that won't clear
- **Codes:** an error code that comes back after a reset
- **No heat:** a unit that runs but won't heat or cool after the checks above
