## Four checks for any heat pump problem

Before you call anyone about a heat pump problem, try these four checks, whatever the symptom:

1. **The remote.** Fresh batteries, the right mode, a set temperature that makes sense, and no timer or weekly schedule switching it off.
2. **The filters.** Open the front panel, slide out the filters, and vacuum or rinse them. Let them dry before refitting.
3. **The outdoor unit.** Nothing blocking the back, sides or front, no leaves or snow packed against it, and no solid ice.
4. **A power reset.** Off at the isolating switch beside the outdoor unit for five minutes, then back on.

**Stay safe:** don't remove panels or covers on either unit, don't poke anything into the fans, and don't hose the outdoor unit while it's running.

## Common heat pump problems, symptom by symptom

Find the symptom your heat pump is showing below, then work through the checks listed under it.

### Not heating, or blowing cool air

- Is it in **heat** mode? Auto and dry modes can blow cool air.
- Is the set temperature **above** the room temperature?
- Is it **defrosting**? On a cold morning, the indoor fan pauses and the outdoor unit steams for a few minutes. Wait it out.
- Are the **filters** clean?
- Is the **outdoor unit** clear and its fan turning?

If it still blows cool air for long periods, the cause is inside the system, such as low refrigerant, a sensor or a valve. That's a job for [fault finding](/heat-pump-troubleshooting/). For cold weather behaviour in more detail, see [how heat pumps cope below zero](/low-temperature-heat-pumps/).

### Won't turn on

- Check the remote batteries and point it straight at the indoor unit from closer.
- Check the circuit breaker on the switchboard and the isolating switch outside.
- Look for a flashing light on the indoor unit. That's the unit reporting its own fault.

A breaker that trips again straight after you reset it is an electrical fault. Leave it off and call.

### Flashing light or error code

Write down the code or the pattern of flashes, then try one power reset. Codes differ between brands and models, so the manual for your model is the place to look them up. If the code comes back, call with it. It tells a technician where to start.

### Water leaking from the indoor unit

Usually a blocked, kinked or poorly sloped condensate drain, or a very dirty coil. Turn the unit off to stop more water collecting, and put a towel underneath. This one needs a technician; left alone it can stain walls and ceilings.

### Ice on the outdoor unit that won't clear

A light frost that comes and goes is normal. A solid block of ice, or ice building up in the base, isn't. Clear the drain path under the unit so meltwater can get away, but don't chip ice off the coil. If it keeps building, the defrost or refrigerant side needs checking.

### Bad smell

A musty smell at start-up means mould and dust on the coil and fan. Clean the filters; if it persists, the coil needs a proper clean. A burning or electrical smell is different: turn it off at the isolating switch and call. More on smells on our [deodorising page](/heat-pump-deodorising/).

### Noisy

Rattles, buzzing and grinding usually come from loose panels, vibration or worn fan bearings. Our [noise guide](/heat-pump-noise-problems-solutions/) explains each sound and what fixes it.

### Room never gets to temperature

- Doors open to the rest of the house?
- Curtains open after dark?
- Fan on low?
- Unit too small for the room?

If the room gets close but never quite there, the temperature sensor may be reading wrong. See [thermostat calibration](/heat-pump-thermostat-calibration/). If it falls well short on cold mornings only, the unit may be undersized; our [sizing guide](/heat-pump-sizing/) explains why.

### Turning on and off often

Short bursts of running and stopping can come from a set temperature close to the room temperature, a unit that's oversized for the room, or the unit sensing warm air near itself and stopping early. Try a slightly higher or lower setting and fan on auto. If it continues, have it checked.

### Power bill has jumped

Check the timer, the mode and the set temperature, then clean the filters. If nothing obvious has changed, our [power usage page](/energy-consumption-monitoring/) lists what else can drive usage up.

## When to stop troubleshooting and call a technician

Turn the heat pump off at the isolating switch and book a visit if you have:

- a burning smell, smoke, sparking or loud electrical buzzing
- water leaking inside
- a breaker that keeps tripping
- ice that won't clear
- an error code that returns after a reset
- a unit that runs but doesn't heat or cool after the checks above
