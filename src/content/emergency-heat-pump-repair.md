## Make it safe before an emergency heat pump repair

See any of the signs below? Switch the heat pump off and make it safe before you call us. A unit that's just stopped is a nuisance. One with an electrical or water problem can be dangerous.

- **Burning smell, smoke, sparks or crackling.** Switch it off at the isolating switch beside the outdoor unit, or at its breaker on the switchboard. Don't turn it back on to see if it was a one-off. If there are flames, get everyone out and call 111.
- **Water pouring from the indoor unit.** Turn it off. Put a towel and a bucket underneath. Move anything plugged in away from the drip. If water is running near a power point, switch that circuit off too.
- **Loud grinding, banging or scraping.** Turn it off. A loose fan blade or a failing bearing gets worse every minute it runs.
- **Hissing from the pipes, with frost or oily marks on the joints.** That points to a refrigerant leak. Switch off and open a window in the room. Keep naked flames away.
- **The outdoor unit is a block of ice.** Don't chip at it or pour boiling water on it. The coil fins bend very easily. Switch off and let it thaw.

Once it's off, leave it off. Resetting a unit with a real fault can turn a small repair into a big one.

## Keep the house warm in the meantime

While the heat pump's off, aim to keep one part of the house warm.

- **One room:** pick a room, close the door and heat just that room with a plug-in heater.
- **Heater safety:** keep portable heaters well clear of curtains, bedding and clothes. Switch them off when you leave the room or go to sleep.
- **Curtains:** close them as soon as the sun goes down. That holds the day's heat in.
- **Other heating:** got a log burner, or another heat pump in a different room? Use that while you wait.

## Faults that look urgent but often aren't

Some things feel like a breakdown but turn out to be normal. Check these first.

- **Steam from the outdoor unit and the indoor fan stops.** That's the defrost cycle on a frosty morning. It clears by itself. Not sure it's defrost? [Heat pump repair](/heat-pump-repair/) describes what normal looks like.
- **It won't start straight after a power cut.** Lots of units wait a few minutes before restarting once the power's back.
- **The remote does nothing.** Try new batteries. Check it isn't set to cool, dry or fan only.
- **A timer has switched it off.** A timer set months ago can still be running.
