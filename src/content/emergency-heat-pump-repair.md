## Make it safe before an emergency heat pump repair

A heat pump that has stopped working is an inconvenience. A heat pump with an electrical or water problem can be a hazard. Before anything else, deal with the signs below.

- **Burning smell, smoke, sparks or crackling.** Switch the unit off at the isolating switch beside the outdoor unit, or at its breaker on the switchboard. Do not turn it back on to see if it was a one-off. If there are flames, get everyone out and call 111.
- **Water pouring from the indoor unit.** Turn it off, put a towel and a bucket underneath, and move anything plugged in away from the drip. If water is running near a power point, switch that circuit off too.
- **Loud grinding, banging or scraping.** Turn it off. A loose fan blade or a failing bearing gets worse every minute it runs.
- **Hissing from the pipes, with frost or oily marks on the joints.** That points to a refrigerant leak. Switch off, open a window in the room and keep naked flames away.
- **The outdoor unit is a block of ice.** Don't chip at it or pour boiling water over it. The coil fins bend very easily. Switch off and let it thaw.

Once it's off, leave it off. Resetting a unit that has a real fault can turn a small repair into a big one.

## Keep the house warm in the meantime

- Pick one room, close its door and heat that room only with a plug-in heater.
- Keep portable heaters well clear of curtains, bedding and clothes, and switch them off when you leave the room or go to sleep.
- Close curtains as soon as the sun goes down to hold the day's heat in.
- If you have a log burner or another heat pump in a different room, use that while you wait.

## Faults that look urgent but often aren't

Some things feel like a breakdown and turn out to be normal. Check these before you assume the worst:

- **Steam from the outdoor unit and the indoor fan stops.** That's the defrost cycle on a frosty morning, and it clears by itself. Our [repair page](/heat-pump-repair/) explains what normal defrost looks like.
- **It won't start straight after a power cut.** Many units wait a few minutes before they restart once the power comes back.
- **The remote does nothing.** Try new batteries, and check it isn't set to cool, dry or fan only.
- **A timer has switched it off.** A timer set months ago can still be running.
