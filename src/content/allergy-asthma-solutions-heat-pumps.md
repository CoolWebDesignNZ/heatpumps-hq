## Why a home's heating matters for allergies and asthma

Cold, damp homes are hard on your lungs. A cold, damp, poorly aired house grows mould and dust mites, and the windows run with condensation. Mould and dust mites can both set off breathing symptoms.

The type of heater matters too. A heat pump burns nothing in the room. Unflued gas, kerosene and LPG heaters put fumes and water vapour straight into the room, so avoid them. Wood and pellet burners still make some smoke, even when they burn well.

We fit heat pumps. We're not health professionals. For advice on asthma or allergies, see your doctor. What we can do is make sure the heat pump helps, not hurts.

## How a heat pump can make allergies worse

A heat pump pulls a lot of room air through its indoor unit. Over months of use:

- **Filters clog.** Dust, pet hair and fibres build up.
- **Dust gets past.** Worn or badly fitted filters let it through. It sticks to the coil and fan.
- **Mould grows.** It likes the damp coil and the drain tray. Most of all after cooling or dry mode in humid weather.
- **The fan spreads it.** Every time the unit starts, it blows air over that build-up and into the room.

The signs are sneezing, itchy eyes or a cough that get worse when it's on, a musty smell when it starts, and dark specks on the louvres.

## Heat pump allergies in Christchurch homes

Some Christchurch homes give a heat pump more dust and damp to deal with than others.

- **Damp homes by the river.** On the low ground near the river in [Beckenham](/beckenham/), homes run colder and damper. Steady warmth keeps walls and windows warm enough that moisture doesn't settle. So we set the unit up to run steadily, not in short evening blasts.
- **Closed-off bedrooms.** In damp [Shirley](/shirley/) homes, the bedrooms behind closed doors are where damp usually starts. A lounge unit won't reach them. A small unit in the bedroom keeps that room warm, and the filters in it need the same care.
- **Busy roads and commercial sites.** Some [Sockburn](/sockburn/) homes back onto them, and dust settles on the indoor filters faster there. Rinse the filters every few weeks in winter, and book a yearly clean.
- **Dry nor'west summers.** Out by the airport in [Harewood](/harewood/), nor'west days carry dust. A unit cooling all summer can clog its filters as fast as it does in winter. Keep rinsing them while it's working hard.
- **Rental flats.** The Healthy Homes standard only covers the main living room. In an [Ilam](/ilam/) flat, a small unit in a cold bedroom helps the tenants and keeps mould down.

## What helps when a heat pump triggers asthma

### Clean filters

The filters behind the front panel are made for you to clean. Do them every few weeks while you're using the heat pump a lot. Vacuum or rinse them. Let them dry fully. Put them back. Don't run the heat pump with the filters out.

### A deep clean of the coil and fan

Cleaning the filters doesn't reach the coil, the fan wheel or the drain tray. That's where mould tends to settle. A professional [heat pump clean](/heat-pump-cleaning/) treats all three. With allergies or asthma in the house, get it cleaned once a year before winter.

### A dry house

A heat pump keeps rooms warm. That cuts condensation on cold surfaces. But the damp still has to go somewhere. Fit extractor fans that vent outside in the bathroom, kitchen and laundry. Air the house on fine days. Get leaks repaired. A heat pump's dry mode can help in some conditions. See [using your heat pump as a dehumidifier](/heat-pump-dehumidification/).

### The right filters for the job

Standard heat pump filters protect the coil and catch bigger dust. They aren't built to take out fine allergens like a HEPA purifier does. Some models have extra filters or air cleaning features. If that matters to you, ask us when you're choosing a model. We'll check the maker's specs for that exact unit.

If allergies are a real problem, put a separate HEPA purifier in the bedroom. Keep the heat pump clean as well. Don't expect the heat pump to do both jobs.

### Keeping dust down

Dust with a damp cloth. Vacuum often, with a vacuum that has a good filter. That helps keep dust mites down.

## A heat pump in the bedroom

In your bedroom or a child's, a small heat pump on a timer keeps the room warm overnight. It doesn't add moisture the way an unflued gas heater does. Point the louvres so the air doesn't blow straight over the bed. Keep the filters clean. Book a clean each year. A [floor console unit](/single-room-heat-pump-installation/) can suit rooms where a high wall unit would blow over the bed.
