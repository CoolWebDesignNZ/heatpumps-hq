## Why a pre-season heat pump check up

A heat pump can sit unused for months and still look fine. The first real test is the first cold snap, and that's when a weak capacitor, a sticky louvre motor or a blocked defrost drain shows up. A tune-up moves that test to a day that suits you.

## What a heat pump tune-up checks

A tune-up checks output, start-up, defrost or drain readiness, filters, settings and sensors.

**Heating or cooling output.** We run the unit in the mode you're about to rely on and measure the air going in and out of the indoor unit. A weak result tells us to look further at airflow or refrigerant before winter, not during it.

**Start-up and ramp-up.** We watch the outdoor unit start, speed up and settle. Hesitation, short cycling or a loud start are early warnings.

**Defrost readiness (before winter).** We look at the outdoor unit's base and drain holes and the ground underneath. On a Canterbury frost night the unit runs defrost cycles to melt ice off its coil, and that water needs somewhere to go.

**Drain and condensate (before summer).** Cooling and dry mode put water through the drain line all day. We prove it runs clear.

**Filters.** Washed and refitted, so the check reflects how the unit should perform.

**Remote, timer and settings.** Batteries, clock, timer and mode. A unit left alone over the off season is still on whatever settings it had last.

**Sensors and room reading.** If the room never feels like the number on the remote, we check where the unit reads temperature from. Persistent mismatches are covered under [thermostat calibration](/heat-pump-thermostat-calibration/).

## Tune-up or full service?

A tune-up is about readiness. A full [heat pump service](/heat-pump-servicing/) is about cleanliness and condition: the coil, the fan wheel, the drain tray and the outdoor coil all get properly cleaned.

- **Serviced in the last year?** A tune-up is usually enough.
- **Not serviced for a year or more?** Do the full service. It includes everything in a tune-up.
- **A smell, a drip or dust on the wall?** You need a clean. See [heat pump cleaning](/heat-pump-cleaning/).

We'll tell you which one your unit needs when you book, and agree the price before we start.

## Seasonal heat pump maintenance in autumn and spring

The autumn check gets the unit ready for heating and the spring check gets it ready for cooling.

**Autumn, for heating.** If you only do one check a year, make it this one. Book it once the summer heat has gone but before the frosts, so there's time to sort anything we find.

**Spring, for cooling.** Worth doing if you cool bedrooms through summer, run a north-facing living area, or use dry mode on muggy days. The focus is the drain, the coil and cooling output.

If you'd like both done each year without having to remember, we can put them on a [service plan](/customised-heat-pump-service-plans/).

## What you can check yourself first

You can do four quick checks yourself before you book:

- Run the unit for twenty minutes in the mode you'll need and feel the airflow.
- Wash the filters.
- Put fresh batteries in the remote and set the clock.
- Clear anything from around the outdoor unit.

If the airflow feels weak or the unit takes a long time to warm the room, book the tune-up rather than waiting for winter.
