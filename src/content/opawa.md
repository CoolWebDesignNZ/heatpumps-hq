## Heat pump installation in Opawa

An install here starts with the room, not the unit. We look at the size of the space, the insulation, the glazing and which way the windows face. A north-facing living room with good insulation needs less capacity than a south-facing one with single glazing.

We fit Mitsubishi Electric, Daikin, Fujitsu and Toshiba units. Wall, floor console, multi-room or ducted. For an older villa with high ceilings and not much wall space, a [floor mounted heat pump](/floor-mounted-heat-pumps/) under a window often works well. For an open plan living area, a [high wall heat pump](/high-wall-heat-pumps/) is the usual choice.

We size every install. If a unit is too small it runs hard and never quite keeps up. If it's too big it cycles on and off and costs more to run. The [heat pump sizing](/heat-pump-sizing/) guide explains how we work out the right capacity.

## Heat pumps for Opawa homes

Opawa is flat and sits beside the Heathcote River. That shapes what a heat pump has to do here. Most of the suburb is on level ground, so access for an install is usually straightforward. The cold comes in winter, with frost on the flat streets and a chill off the water.

- **Villas and bungalows from the early 1900s.** Many Opawa streets have older wooden homes with timber floors, high ceilings and single glazing. These lose heat through the floor and ceiling, so they need more capacity than the floor area suggests.
- **State houses and 1950s to 1970s homes.** Solidly built, often with a single living room and separate bedrooms. One high wall unit in the living area, plus a second unit or a multi-room system for the bedrooms, is a common setup.
- **Newer homes and townhouses.** These hold heat well. One unit often covers an open plan living and dining area. The outdoor unit has to fit a narrow side yard with clear air around it.

## Frost and cold mornings in Opawa

Opawa sits on flat ground away from the coast, and winter mornings here frost. The outdoor unit can ice up overnight, and most modern heat pumps run a defrost cycle to clear it. On the coldest days a standard model loses some output while it defrosts. A [cold climate heat pump](/cold-climate-heat-pumps/) holds its heating output better at low temperatures, so it's worth considering if the house is hard to keep warm.

If your current unit struggles on frosty mornings, a [low temperature heat pump](/low-temperature-heat-pumps/) may suit better than a like-for-like replacement.

## Damp and the river

Opawa sits beside the Heathcote River, and some homes here feel damp in winter. A heat pump that keeps the main rooms at a steady temperature cuts condensation on windows and walls. On mild, muggy days, dry mode pulls moisture out of the air without heating the room much. If damp is a persistent problem, see [heat pump dehumidification](/heat-pump-dehumidification/). You still need ventilation and insulation as well.

If anyone in the house has asthma or allergies, an [allergy and asthma solution](/allergy-asthma-solutions-heat-pumps/) with good filtration is worth discussing at the quote.
