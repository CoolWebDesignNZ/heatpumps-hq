## What remote monitoring means for a home heat pump

For a home heat pump, remote monitoring means a Wi-Fi adaptor in the indoor unit linked to the manufacturer's app on your phone. Through it you can see:

- whether the unit is on or off, and which mode it's in
- the temperature you've set and the room temperature the unit is reading
- the schedules and timers that are set
- energy use, on some models and apps

It works from anywhere with an internet connection, so you can check on the house from work or while you're away. Which app you use depends on the brand. Each brand we install has its own [smart heat pump](/smart-heat-pump-systems/) Wi-Fi control and app.

## What heat pump monitoring can't tell you

An app reports what the unit's own sensors and controls know. It doesn't measure refrigerant pressure, check the wiring, look at the drain or listen to the fan bearings. A heat pump can show normal readings in the app and still have a slow refrigerant leak or a blocked drain.

So remote diagnostics on a home system is a head start, not a full diagnosis. The tests that confirm a fault are done at the unit, as part of our [fault finding](/heat-pump-troubleshooting/).

## How remote diagnostics help us fix a heat pump fault

When something goes wrong, the app often holds clues that are gone by the time anyone looks at the unit:

- **When it stopped.** A unit that stopped at 3am on the coldest night of the week tells a different story to one that failed the moment it was switched on.
- **What the room temperature did.** A room that slowly stopped warming over a fortnight points towards a gradual problem such as a leak or a blocked coil, rather than a sudden part failure.
- **Whether a setting changed.** A schedule, a temperature limit or a mode change made in the app by someone else in the house can look exactly like a fault.
- **Any fault notice.** If the app shows an error or alert, take a screenshot before you reset anything.

Send us those screenshots when you book, with the brand and model number. It often means we arrive with a clearer idea of where to start and the right part in the van.

## Getting monitoring working on your heat pump

Mitsubishi Electric, Daikin and Fujitsu all offer Wi-Fi control on selected models, and some existing units can have an adaptor added later. What makes it reliable:

- **Signal at the indoor unit.** The adaptor sits inside the heat pump, often high on an outside wall far from the router. A weak signal there causes dropouts.
- **The right network band.** Many heat pump adaptors use 2.4 GHz only. Mesh systems and new routers sometimes need a setting changed so the adaptor can join.
- **Re-pairing after changes.** A new router, a new Wi-Fi name or a new password means connecting the adaptor again through the app.
- **One account for the house.** Set the app up under a shared household email, so whoever handles the heating can get into it.

If your heat pump is compatible, we can check it and set up the adaptor while we're there for a service or repair.

## Monitoring energy use

Where an app shows energy figures, they're worth checking month to month. A big jump with no change in how you use the unit can be the first sign of a dirty coil, a failing part or a leak. For a closer look at running costs, [energy consumption monitoring](/energy-consumption-monitoring/) tracks use over time.
