## Choosing heat pumps for Richmond homes

Richmond is a flat, inner suburb east of the city centre, between the Avon and the edge of Shirley. Most of the houses are old. Villas and bungalows from the early 1900s sit on small sections, often with a narrow gap to the neighbour. Newer townhouses have gone up on the subdivided back sections. That mix decides most of the heat pump work here.

- **Villas and bungalows.** Timber floors, high ceilings in places, single glazing in the front rooms. They lose heat through the floor and ceiling, so they need more capacity than their floor area suggests.
- **Small cottages.** Not much wall space. A high wall unit in the living area works, or a floor console under a front window.
- **Townhouses and back sections.** Often well insulated. One high wall unit covers an open plan living area. The harder part is outside, where the outdoor unit has to sit in a narrow side yard.
- **Rental properties.** Many Richmond houses are rented. A correctly sized unit and a yearly service keep the running costs predictable for the tenant and the owner.

## Heat pump installation in Richmond

The install itself is usually a day's work. The planning is what takes care. Richmond's narrow sections mean the outdoor unit often goes down the gap between houses, which is also where the cold easterly funnels. We look for a spot with clear air around the unit, out of the worst of the wind, and far enough from the boundary to meet the noise limits. Bracket-mounted units keep it off the ground and clear of the damp.

Inside, the unit goes where the heat will reach the rooms you use. In a villa with a long hall and closed doors, that can mean a second unit or a small multi-room setup rather than one big unit working harder.

We check ceiling and underfloor insulation at the quote. Insulating first can mean a smaller unit that costs less to run. If you're weighing up a single unit against a multi-room system, [single and multi-room](/single-multi-room/) sets out the difference.

## Servicing heat pumps in Richmond

A yearly [service](/heat-pump-servicing/) keeps a heat pump running at its best. We clean the filters and the indoor coil, check the outdoor unit and the refrigerant charge, and test the drainage. In Richmond the outdoor unit often sits in a narrow gap where leaves and dust collect, so we check the coil for build-up.

If your house is rented, a [customised service plan](/customised-heat-pump-service-plans/) spreads the cost across the year and keeps the reminders coming. Tenants can check the [heating tool](https://tools.tenancy.govt.nz/heating-tool) to see what the Healthy Homes standards require.

## Heat pump repair in Richmond

When a heat pump stops heating, the cause is often simple: a blocked filter, a tripped breaker, a drain that's frozen or blocked. We check those first. If it needs a part, we source the right one for the brand and model. [Troubleshooting](/heat-pump-troubleshooting/) covers the checks you can do before you ring.

Older units in Richmond villas are sometimes past their best. If a repair is a big one, we'll tell you what a [replacement](/heat-pump-replacement-christchurch/) would involve so you can weigh it up.

## The Richmond climate

Richmond is flat and open to the east, so the cold easterly off the coast reaches it. Winter mornings bring frost on the flat ground. A heat pump sized for a colder room runs less hard on those mornings and holds its heat better. In summer the same unit cools the house on the hot nor'west days.

Because the sections are close, noise matters. The council's District Plan sets the limit at your neighbour's boundary, and we check placement against it at the quote. The [boundary noise rules](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/) explain how it's measured.
