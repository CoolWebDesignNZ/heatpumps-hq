## Heat pumps for Lincoln homes, old and new

The right heat pump for a Lincoln home depends mostly on how old the house is and how its rooms are laid out.

- **Newer subdivision homes** are usually well insulated and double glazed, with one large open plan living area. A single correctly sized high wall unit can heat the living, dining and kitchen space. Bedrooms can have a second, smaller unit, or the whole house can go ducted if you want every room warm.
- **Older bungalows near the town centre** lose more heat through the ceiling, the floor and single glazed windows, and their rooms are smaller and closed off. A unit in the lounge won't reach bedrooms down the hall, so these homes often suit a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) with a unit in each room you use. It pays to check the ceiling and underfloor insulation first.
- **Farmhouses and lifestyle block houses** out toward Tai Tapu and Prebbleton tend to be bigger, with several living areas and long hallways. They usually need several wall units or a ducted system, covered below.

At the quote we measure each room, look at the insulation and windows, and size every unit for a frosty Lincoln morning, not an average day.

## Heat pumps on Lincoln's frosty open ground

Lincoln is on open, flat land, and still winter nights bring hard frosts. That's when a heat pump works hardest and when the outdoor unit runs its defrost cycle. We mount it on raised feet or a bracket so the melt water drains away instead of freezing into a block of ice beneath it, and keep it clear of spots where frost lingers longest, such as a shaded corner against a south wall.

Capacity matters as well. A unit sized for an average day struggles on a cold morning, so we size for the conditions you actually get. Our guide to [cold climate heat pumps](/cold-climate-heat-pumps/) explains what to look for.

## Heat pump installation on Lincoln lifestyle blocks

On a lifestyle block the house is often larger, and it may have a sleepout or a granny flat as well. Some owners want to move off a log burner or an old electric heater and want the whole house warm, not just the lounge. Options include:

- **Several wall units** on one or more outdoor units, one per main room.
- **A ducted system** where there's good ceiling space, heating every room through ceiling vents.
- **A separate small unit** for a sleepout or office.

We'll walk through the house with you and explain what each option suits.

## Selwyn noise rules in Lincoln

Lincoln is in Selwyn District, not Christchurch City, so the Selwyn District Plan applies to any noise from the outdoor unit. On a rural block the neighbours may be well away, but in town the house next door can be close.
