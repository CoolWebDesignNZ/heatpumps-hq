## What a ceiling cassette heat pump is and who it suits

A ceiling cassette heat pump is an indoor unit set into the ceiling. Only the grille shows, flush with the ceiling line. It draws air up through the centre of the grille, heats or cools it, and blows it out through louvres on several sides. It joins to an outdoor unit by pipes and a drain run through the ceiling space.

It suits rooms where there's no clear wall for a unit, and rooms where you don't want a box on the wall. It also suits large open plan spaces, because the air comes out in several directions from one point rather than from one wall.

## Where a ceiling cassette goes in the room

The usual spot is the centre of the room or as close to it as the ceiling allows. From there the air reaches every side.

- **Clear of beams and joists.** The cassette needs a clear opening in the ceiling lining and space between the framing above it.
- **Away from downlights.** Lights close to the grille get hot air blown at them, and the cassette needs its own clearance.
- **Clear of the curtains.** Long drapes below the grille catch the air and send it back up.
- **Not over a bed or a desk.** Air blowing straight down on you is uncomfortable. In a bedroom, keep the cassette over the open floor.
- **A short pipe route.** The pipes and drain run through the ceiling to an outside wall, so the closer the outdoor unit, the simpler the run.

In an open plan kitchen and living area, we aim to put it over the open floor between the two, so both get the air.

## Ceiling cassette heat pump or high wall unit

Both heat and cool. The difference is where the unit sits and where the air goes.

- **Wall space.** A cassette needs none. A wall unit needs a clear stretch of wall up high.
- **Air spread.** A cassette blows air out in several directions from the centre of the room. A wall unit blows from one wall and has to push across the room.
- **Ceiling space.** A cassette needs room above the ceiling for the unit, the pipes and the drain. A wall unit needs none.
- **Installation.** A wall unit is quicker to fit. A cassette takes longer because of the ceiling work.
- **Furniture.** A cassette leaves the walls free for furniture, art and shelves. A wall unit takes the top of one wall.
- **Filter access.** A cassette's filters come out through the grille, so you reach up or use a step. A wall unit's filters lift out at head height.

For a room with clear wall space up high and a standard ceiling, read about [high wall heat pumps](/high-wall-heat-pumps/). For a room with a high stud and low wall space, a [floor mounted heat pump](/floor-mounted-heat-pumps/) may suit better. And where you want one unit to heat several separate rooms through ceiling vents, read about [ducted heat pumps](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/).

## What a ceiling cassette heat pump needs above the ceiling

This is the part that decides whether a cassette will work in your home. We check it before anything is ordered.

- **Depth.** The cassette body sits above the ceiling lining, so the ceiling space has to be deep enough for the unit and its frame.
- **Pipe route.** The refrigerant pipes run from the cassette through the ceiling space to an outside wall and down to the outdoor unit.
- **Condensate drain.** The water the unit pulls out of the air has to run away. Where the ceiling space allows a fall to outside, gravity does it. Where it doesn't, we fit a small pump in the ceiling space.
- **Access.** We need to get into the ceiling space to fit the unit and to service it later. A ceiling hatch close by helps.
- **Framing.** The ceiling framing may need trimming or adding to for the opening. We plan that as part of the job.

In a single storey home with a pitched roof, there's usually enough room. In a two storey home, the ceiling space under the upper floor is tighter, and we check it carefully.

## What changes the price of a ceiling cassette heat pump install

The cassette is only part of the price. The rest comes down to the ceiling and the route to the outdoor unit.

- **The size of unit.** A large open plan room needs more heat output, and a bigger cassette costs more.
- **The ceiling space.** A deep, open ceiling space is quick to work in. A tight one, or one with little access, takes longer.
- **The drain.** A gravity drain is simpler. A condensate pump adds a part and a little wiring.
- **The pipe run.** The further the outdoor unit from the cassette, the more pipe and cover.
- **The switchboard.** If there's no room for a new circuit, the board needs work first.

We look at each of these, and the other [installation cost factors](/heat-pump-installation-cost-christchurch/), when we visit, and they're all in the quote.

## Replacing an old ceiling cassette heat pump

An older cassette that's failing can usually be swapped in the same ceiling opening, if the new unit fits the space and the old pipe route. We still check the size against the room, because a room that's since had insulation or double glazing added may need a smaller unit than before.

We look at whether the new cassette can use the old pipes and drain, and whether the ceiling space still has room for it. If the old unit is worth fixing, we'll say so and point you to [heat pump repair](/heat-pump-repair/).
