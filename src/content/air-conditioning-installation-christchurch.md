## Heat pump or air conditioning?

In Christchurch they're the same thing. The split systems sold here are reverse cycle heat pumps. The units that heat the house in July cool it in January. That's why you get shown heat pumps when you ask for air con. In cooling mode the refrigerant carries heat out of the room and dumps it outside. That's why the outdoor unit blows warm air in summer.

So you're not choosing between an air conditioner and a heat pump. The real question is whether your system is sized and placed for cooling as well as heating.

## Christchurch rooms that overheat

Some rooms are a problem in summer even when the house is fine in winter.

- **Upstairs bedrooms** under a roof that soaks up the afternoon sun
- **Living areas with big west facing windows**, which cop the full late afternoon heat
- **New open plan homes** with lots of glass and good insulation, which hold heat in summer just like in winter
- **Home offices** with computers on all day

These rooms need enough cooling for the heat coming through the glass and roof. Floor area alone won't tell you. We check the window size, which way they face, the shading and the ceiling insulation. Then we pick a unit.

## Placing an aircon unit for cooling

Cool air sinks. That makes a high wall unit a good spot for cooling. It pushes air out across the ceiling and lets it fall through the room. We aim the airflow across the room, not straight at a bed or a couch. A cold draught is uncomfortable, even on a hot day.

The condensate drain matters more for cooling than heating. In cooling mode the indoor unit pulls water out of the air. That water has to fall away to a drain point or outside. It can't have low spots where it sits. Get the drain right at install and that's one less reason for a callout later.

## Dry mode and humid days

Lots of heat pumps have a dry mode. It takes moisture out of the air with less cooling. Use it on muggy, grey days when the room feels clammy rather than hot. When to use it, and how it differs from cooling, is under [heat pump dehumidification](/heat-pump-dehumidification/).

## Air conditioning for more than one room

Several rooms getting too hot? You've got the same choices as for heating.

- A unit in each room
- A [multi room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) with one outdoor unit
- A [ducted system](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/) with a vent in every room

Ducted cooling suits upstairs bedrooms well. The ducts and indoor unit can sit in the roof space right above them.
