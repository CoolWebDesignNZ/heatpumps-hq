## Heat pump or air conditioning?

In Christchurch they are the same thing. The split systems sold here are reverse cycle heat pumps: the same outdoor and indoor units that heat the house in July cool it in January, which is why you'll be shown heat pumps when you ask for air conditioning. In cooling mode the refrigerant carries heat out of the room and releases it outside, which is why the outdoor unit blows warm air in summer.

So the question is not whether to buy an air conditioner or a heat pump. It is whether the system you choose has been sized and placed for cooling as well as heating.

## Christchurch rooms that overheat

Some rooms are a problem in summer even when the house is comfortable in winter:

- **Upstairs bedrooms** under a roof that soaks up the afternoon sun
- **Living areas with big west facing windows**, which take the full heat of the late afternoon
- **New open plan homes** with a lot of glass and good insulation, which hold heat as well in summer as they do in winter
- **Home offices** with computers running all day

These rooms need enough cooling output for the heat that comes in through the glass and roof, not just the floor area. We check window size, orientation, shading and ceiling insulation before choosing a unit.

## Placing an aircon unit for cooling

Cool air sinks, so a high wall unit is well placed for cooling: it pushes air out across the ceiling and lets it fall through the room. We aim the airflow across the space rather than straight at a bed or a couch, because a cold draught is uncomfortable even on a hot day.

The condensate drain matters more for cooling than for heating. In cooling mode the indoor unit removes moisture from the air, and that water has to fall away to a drain point or outside with no low spots where it can sit. A drain run properly at install is one less reason for a callout later.

## Dry mode and humid days

Many heat pumps have a dry mode that lowers the moisture in the air with less cooling. It is useful on muggy, overcast days when the room feels clammy rather than hot. When to use it, and how it differs from cooling, is covered in [heat pump dehumidification](/heat-pump-dehumidification/).

## Air conditioning for more than one room

If several rooms overheat, you have the same choices as for heating: a unit in each room, a [multi room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) with one outdoor unit, or a [ducted system](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/) with a vent in every room. Ducted cooling suits upstairs bedrooms well, because the ducts and indoor unit can sit in the roof space above them.
