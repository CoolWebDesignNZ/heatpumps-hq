## What the smell is telling you

| Smell | Likely cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Musty, damp, old socks | Mould and bacteria on the coil, fan wheel or drain tray | Book a clean |
| Dusty, slightly scorched, on the first cold day | Dust burning off after months unused | Run it, open a window, see if it fades |
| Hot plastic, electrical burning | Overheating wiring or component | Turn off at the isolator and call us |
| Sour or vinegary | Bacterial build-up, often in the drain tray | Book a clean |
| Sewage or rotten | Drain line or nearby plumbing vent letting air back | Book a check |
| Cigarette smoke, cooking | Odours absorbed into dirty filters and coil | Clean the filters, then book a clean if it stays |

An unusual sharp chemical smell around the indoor or outdoor unit is worth a call too. Don't try to find the source by opening the unit.

## Why heat pumps start to smell

The indoor coil gets cold and wet whenever the unit cools or dehumidifies. Dust that gets past the filters sticks to the wet fins. Warmth, water and dust are all mould needs. When the unit starts, the fan blows air straight across that growth and into the room.

Smells are often worst in spring and early summer, when the unit switches from heating to cooling and the coil gets wet again. A unit that has sat unused for months can also smell stale on its first run.

## How we deodorise a heat pump

We treat smells by removing what causes them:

1. **Find the source.** We open the unit and look at the filters, coil, fan wheel and drain tray. If the unit looks clean, we look beyond it, at the drain line outlet, the room and the ceiling space.
2. **Clean the coil and fan wheel.** These hold most of the growth. The unit is bagged, cleaned and rinsed, as described on our [heat pump cleaning](/heat-pump-cleaning/) page.
3. **Clear the drain.** The tray and line are flushed so water doesn't sit and go sour.
4. **Replace worn filters.** Torn mesh filters and spent deodorising filters are replaced where the model uses them.
5. **Dry the unit out.** It runs in fan or heat mode after the clean so the coil isn't left wet.

If the smell is electrical, it's a fault rather than a clean, and it's handled as a [repair](/heat-pump-repair/).

## Stopping the smell coming back

- **Wash the filters regularly.** Less dust on the coil means less for mould to feed on.
- **Dry the coil after cooling.** Some units have a setting that runs the fan briefly after cooling or dry mode to dry the coil. If yours doesn't, run it in fan mode for a short while before turning it off.
- **Keep the drain clear.** If the drain outlet outside stops dripping in cooling mode, get it checked.
- **Service it yearly.** A yearly clean stops growth building up to the point you can smell it. See [heat pump servicing](/heat-pump-servicing/).

## When it isn't the heat pump

If the heat pump is clean and the room still smells damp, the moisture may be in the house: a wet subfloor, a leak, or not enough ventilation. A heat pump moves air around the room; it can't remove a damp smell that's coming from the building.
