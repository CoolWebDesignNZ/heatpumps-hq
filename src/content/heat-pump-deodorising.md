## What a heat pump smell is telling you

Each smell points to a different cause. An electrical burning smell means turn it off at the isolator.

| Smell | Likely cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Musty, damp, old socks | Mould and bacteria on the coil, fan wheel or drain tray | Book a clean |
| Dusty, a bit scorched, on the first cold day | Dust burning off after months unused | Run it, open a window, see if it fades |
| Hot plastic, electrical burning | Wiring or a part overheating | Turn off at the isolator and call us |
| Sour or vinegary | Bacteria, often in the drain tray | Book a clean |
| Sewage or rotten | Drain line or nearby plumbing vent letting air back | Book a check |
| Cigarette smoke, cooking | Smells soaked into dirty filters and coil | Clean the filters, then book a clean if it stays |

A strange, sharp chemical smell round the indoor or outdoor unit is worth a call too. Don't open the unit to hunt for it.

## Why heat pumps start to smell

The indoor coil gets cold and wet whenever the unit cools or dries the air. Dust that gets past the filters sticks to the wet fins. Warmth, water and dust. That's all mould needs. When the unit starts, the fan blows air straight over that growth and into the room.

Smells are often worst in spring and early summer. That's when the unit swaps from heating to cooling, and the coil gets wet again. A unit that's sat unused for months can smell stale on its first run, too.

## How we remove a heat pump odour

We get rid of the cause.

1. **Find the source.** We open the unit. We look at the filters, coil, fan wheel and drain tray. If the unit looks clean, we look further. The drain outlet, the room, the ceiling space.
2. **Clean the coil and fan wheel.** Most of the growth is here. We bag the unit, clean it and rinse it. It's the same as a full [heat pump clean](/heat-pump-cleaning/).
3. **Clear the drain.** We flush the tray and line, so water doesn't sit there and go sour.
4. **Replace worn filters.** Torn mesh filters. Worn-out deodorising filters, if your model uses them.
5. **Dry it out.** We run it on fan or heat after the clean. The coil isn't left wet.

An electrical smell is a fault, not a cleaning job. We handle it as a [repair](/heat-pump-repair/).

## Stopping a musty heat pump smell coming back

Once it's clean, four habits keep the musty smell away.

- **Wash the filters often.** Less dust on the coil means less food for mould.
- **Dry the coil after cooling.** Some units have a setting that runs the fan for a bit after cooling or dry mode. If yours doesn't, run it on fan for a short while before you turn it off.
- **Keep the drain clear.** In cooling mode, the drain outside should drip. If it stops, get it checked.
- **Service it every year.** A yearly clean stops growth building up to where you can smell it. See [heat pump servicing](/heat-pump-servicing/).

## When it isn't the heat pump

Heat pump clean, but the room still smells damp? The damp may be in the house. A wet subfloor, a leak, or not enough fresh air. A heat pump moves air round the room. It can't get rid of a damp smell coming from the building. Next steps are [air quality testing](/air-quality-testing/), and running the heat pump in [dry mode](/heat-pump-dehumidification/).
