## What a floor mounted heat pump is and who it suits

A floor mounted heat pump, often called a floor console, is a low indoor unit. It sits against the wall at skirting level, about knee height, and joins to an outdoor unit by pipes through the wall or floor. It draws air in, heats or cools it, and blows it out low into the room.

It suits rooms that a wall unit struggles with. Villas and cottages with a high stud. Rooms where windows, doors or a fireplace take up the walls. Rooms with sloped ceilings. And it suits you if you want warmth close to where you sit, and filters you can reach from the floor.

## Where a floor console heat pump goes in the room

The usual spot is under a window. That's often the only clear stretch of wall low down, and the console warms the cold air coming off the glass before it spreads across the floor.

- **Open floor in front.** A couch or chair pushed up close blows the warm air straight back into the unit.
- **Curtains clear of the outlets.** Long drapes over the console trap the heat behind them.
- **A short pipe route.** An outside wall behind the console is simplest. Otherwise, the pipes go down through the floor and under the house.
- **Not behind a door.** A door that swings open over the unit blocks it.

In a lounge, we aim to put it on the coldest wall, facing into where you sit.

Christchurch's older housing is where a console earns its place. These are the homes we fit them in most.

- **Character villas.** In [St Albans](/st-albans/) and [Edgeware](/edgeware/), villas have high studs, sash windows and rooms that close off from each other. Heat rises to the ceiling. A floor console sits low under a sash window and pushes warm air out where you sit.

- **Hill cottages.** In [Lyttelton](/lyttelton/), timber cottages and villas have tall ceilings and walls with little or no insulation. A console under a window suits rooms where a fireplace or bookshelves take up the walls.
- **Older beach houses.** In [Sumner](/sumner/), older homes with high ceilings or not much wall space often suit a console under a window.

## Floor mounted heat pump or high wall unit

Both heat and cool. The difference is where the air comes from.

- **Heating evenness.** Warm air rises. Starting it at floor level keeps your feet warm and the room even, which matters most under a tall stud. A wall unit has to push warm air down from above.
- **Cooling spread.** A console cools, but cool air sinks and stays low. A wall unit spreads cooling through the room better.
- **Wall space.** A console needs a clear stretch of wall low down. A wall unit needs a clear stretch up high.
- **Windows.** Under a window is a good spot for a console. Above a window is a poor spot for a wall unit.
- **Furniture.** A console takes some floor space and needs open air in front. A wall unit leaves the floor free.
- **Filter access.** A console's filters slide out at knee height, no step stool needed.
- **Noise at head height.** A console runs beside you, not above you. On a low fan, both are quiet. Next to a reading chair, you may notice a console more.

For a room with a standard ceiling and good wall space up high, read about [high wall heat pumps](/high-wall-heat-pumps/).

## What changes the price of a floor console install

The main factors:

- the size of unit the room needs, based on its volume
- whether the pipes go through the wall or down through the floor
- how much room there is under the house to run them
- the distance to the outdoor unit and how it's mounted
- whether the switchboard or circuit needs work

## Replacing an old floor standing heat pump

An older console that's failing can usually be swapped in the same spot. That spot is often right, since there's only so much clear wall under a window. We still check the size against the room. An old villa that's since had insulation or double glazing added may need a smaller unit than before.

We look at whether the new console can use the old pipe route through the floor or wall. A newer model may need a different pipe size or its own circuit, and we'll tell you before you agree to anything.
