## Choosing heat pumps for Waltham homes

Waltham sits just south of the central city, between the railway and the river. The housing is a mix. There are villas and workers' cottages from the early 1900s, some state houses from the middle of the century, and newer townhouses on the sections that were rebuilt after the earthquakes. Most of the suburb is flat, which makes the outdoor unit easy to place. The work is in the houses themselves.

- **Villas and workers' cottages.** Timber floors, single glazing and little wall or ceiling insulation in many. They lose heat fast, so they need more capacity than their floor area suggests. A high wall unit in the living room works well where there's wall space. A floor console under a window suits a cottage with low ceilings and nowhere to hang a unit high.
- **Mid-century state houses.** Better insulated than the villas, and often with a heat pump already fitted. When the old unit gives out, a [replacement](/heat-pump-replacement-christchurch/) sized to the room is usually all that's needed.
- **Newer townhouses.** These hold heat well. One high wall unit often heats an open plan living area. The harder part is outside, where the unit has to fit a narrow side yard with clear air around it and away from the neighbour.

## Heat pump installation in Waltham

We size every install on the house, not the floor plan. In a Waltham villa, that means checking the ceiling and underfloor insulation and the age of the windows before we choose a unit. Insulating first can mean a smaller heat pump that costs less to run. We look at the layout too. A [single room unit](/single-room-heat-pump-installation/) is often enough for a cottage. A larger open plan living area may want a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) or a [ducted unit](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/) if the ceilings allow.

Waltham is flat, so outdoor units usually go on a wall bracket or a ground stand at the side of the house. Sections are narrow and neighbours are close. We check the [boundary noise rules](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/) before we choose the spot, and we keep the unit clear of the fence line.

Mitsubishi Electric, Daikin, Fujitsu and Toshiba are the brands we fit. If you're weighing them up, our [brands comparison](/heat-pump-brands-comparison/) sets out the differences.

## Servicing heat pumps in Waltham

A heat pump in Waltham works harder than one in a newer suburb. Older houses are dustier, and many have less insulation, so the unit runs longer to hold the temperature. A yearly [service](/heat-pump-servicing/) keeps the filter, indoor coil and condensate drain clear. It also catches worn parts before they fail.

If the house is close to the railway or one of the busy roads, clean the filter every few weeks through winter. Dust and fine grit build up faster there. We can set up a [service plan](/customised-heat-pump-service-plans/) so the yearly visit happens without you having to remember it.

## Heat pump repair in Waltham

We repair heat pumps on every major brand, Panasonic included. Common faults in older Waltham houses are a blocked condensate drain, a dirty coil that cuts output, and worn fan or control parts. We test the unit, tell you what's wrong and what it will take to put right, and get on with it. If the unit has stopped altogether, an [emergency callout](/emergency-heat-pump-repair/) gets it looked at sooner.

When a unit is old and the repair is more than it's worth, we'll say so. A [replacement](/heat-pump-replacement-christchurch/) sized to the room is often the better spend.

## Winter in Waltham

Waltham is flat and inland, so it gets the frosts that come with still, clear winter nights. It's nothing like the hill suburbs, but the outdoor unit will ice up on cold mornings. Every heat pump we fit has a defrost cycle that clears it. If the unit ices up and stays iced, that's a fault worth a look, not something to chip off by hand.

Cold nights are when a heat pump earns its keep. A unit that's the right size for the room holds the temperature without running flat out, which keeps the power bill down. If yours struggles on frosty mornings, the unit may be undersized or low on refrigerant, and that's worth a call.
