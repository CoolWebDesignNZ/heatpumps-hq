## What Burwood homes need from a heat pump installation

Burwood has two quite different kinds of housing. There are newer subdivisions of brick and tile homes with insulated ceilings, double glazing and open plan living, and there are older family houses on bigger sections that have had insulation added in stages. The right heat pump for one is rarely right for the other.

In a newer Burwood home, the living area usually holds heat well once it's warm. A correctly sized high wall unit can run gently for most of the day instead of working hard morning and night. Oversizing just adds cost and short cycling, so we work the capacity out from your rooms, glazing and insulation rather than from the floor area alone.

In an older Burwood house, heat escapes faster through single glazing and older walls. The unit has to be sized for that, and placement matters more: a unit at the far end of a long lounge will leave the other end cold.

## Heating bedrooms and hallways in Burwood

The usual complaint in Burwood homes is a warm lounge and cold bedrooms. You have three ways to fix it:

- A second **high wall unit** in the main bedroom or a hallway.
- A **multi-room system**, with one outdoor unit running two to several indoor units.
- A **ducted system** with a vent in each room, which suits homes with enough ceiling space. Compare them on our [ducted vs split](/ducted-vs-split-heat-pumps-christchurch/) page.

## Outdoor units on Burwood sections

Burwood sections are mostly flat, which makes the outdoor unit easy to place. We set it on a level pad or a wall bracket with clearance below for defrost water, and keep it away from bedroom windows next door. Christchurch measures heat pump noise at the neighbour's boundary, not by distance, and our [boundary rules guide](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/) explains what that means for where the unit goes.
