## What Burwood homes need from a heat pump installation

Burwood has two kinds of houses. They need different heat pump setups.

- **Newer subdivision homes.** These are brick and tile, with insulated ceilings, double glazing and open plan living. Once the living area is warm, it stays warm. So a high wall unit sized right can tick over gently all day. It doesn't have to work hard morning and night. A unit that's too big costs more and short cycles, switching on and off. We work out the size from your rooms, glazing and insulation, not just the floor area.
- **Older family houses on bigger sections.** Insulation has usually gone in bit by bit. Heat still gets out faster through single glazing and older walls. The same room needs more capacity. Where the unit goes matters more too. A unit at the far end of a long lounge leaves the other end cold. So we mount it where the air can run the length of the room.

In both kinds of house, a single lounge unit misses the bedrooms down the hall.

## Heating bedrooms and hallways in Burwood

The usual complaint in Burwood is a warm lounge and cold bedrooms. You've got three ways to fix it:

- **A second high wall unit** in the main bedroom or a hallway. It works on its own, with its own outdoor unit.
- **A multi-room system.** One outdoor unit runs two or more indoor units, so there's only one box outside.
- **A ducted system.** There's a vent in each room. It suits homes with enough ceiling space. See how they compare in [ducted vs split heat pumps](/ducted-vs-split-heat-pumps-christchurch/).

## Outdoor units on Burwood sections

Most Burwood sections are flat. That makes the outdoor unit easy to place. We set it on a level pad or a wall bracket, with room underneath for defrost water. Next door's bedroom windows stay well clear of it. Christchurch measures heat pump noise at the neighbour's boundary, not by distance. So the unit goes where it stays within the [boundary noise rules](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/).
