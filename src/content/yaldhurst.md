## Lifestyle blocks on the plains

Past the edge of the suburbs, Yaldhurst opens out into lifestyle blocks and larger homes on bigger sections, with a few newer subdivisions closer to town. Houses here are often bigger than a city home and more exposed. There's nothing between them and the weather coming across the plains.

That exposure is the main thing that shapes a heat pump install in Yaldhurst. Two problems in particular: hard frosts on still, clear nights and the dry nor'west wind.

## Sizing for the cold

A heat pump moves heat from the outside air into the house. The colder it is outside, the less heat it can deliver, which is the opposite of what you need on a frosty morning. So we size for the cold end of the range, not for a mild day. For an exposed house, we also look at units designed to hold their output at low temperatures; see [cold climate heat pumps](/cold-climate-heat-pumps/).

Larger homes in Yaldhurst often suit [ducted heating](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/), with a vent in each room and the indoor unit out of sight in the roof space. Others are better served by two or three wall units in the rooms that are used every day.

## Where the outdoor unit goes on an open section

On an open section, the outdoor unit needs shelter from the prevailing wind and a raised base clear of frost and long grass. It also needs somewhere for defrost water to drain without freezing into a sheet of ice on a path. On a rural block, we can usually find a spot well away from the neighbours. In the subdivisions, the Christchurch District Plan noise limits at the neighbour's site apply, as they do anywhere in the city; see the [boundary rules guide](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/).

## Heating more than the house

Lifestyle properties often have a sleepout, a home office in a converted garage or a studio away from the main house. Each of these can have its own compact heat pump on its own outdoor unit, sized for that one room. It's usually simpler than extending a system from the house.

## Dust and servicing

Wind-blown dust from paddocks and shingle driveways builds up on outdoor coils and indoor filters. A clogged coil reduces heat output just when you need it. A yearly [service](/maintenance-servicing/) before winter keeps a rural heat pump running as it should.
