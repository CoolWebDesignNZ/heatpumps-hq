## Heat pumps for exposed Brooklands homes

Brooklands is on the northern edge of Christchurch, near Brooklands Lagoon and the mouth of the Waimakariri River. It was badly affected by the earthquakes and much of the land became residential red zone, so the homes that remain stand in open ground, with wind off the coast and damp air from the lagoon. Wind and damp decide the heat pump here more than anything else.

- **Older houses, some of them repaired,** have timber walls with limited insulation. Wind strips heat from them faster than from a sheltered house, so they need a unit sized for a cold, windy morning, not a still one.
- **The small number of newer homes** are better sealed and insulated, so a single wall unit can heat the living area without being oversized.
- **The outdoor unit** on an exposed site takes wind, salt and grit straight onto its coil, so its position matters more here than in most of the city.

Each of these is covered below, starting with where the outdoor unit goes.

## Placing a heat pump in the Brooklands wind

Placing a heat pump in Brooklands means keeping the outdoor unit out of the wind, because on an open site wind blows straight into it, working against its fan and pushing salt and grit onto the coil. We walk around the house before choosing a position, looking for a wall that's sheltered but still has open air in front. Where every wall is exposed, a slatted screen set back from the unit can soften the wind without boxing it in.

## Damp air and soft ground near the lagoon

Low ground near the lagoon can be soft and wet through winter. An outdoor unit sitting low, or on a pad that settles, collects water and drains badly during defrost. We raise it on a bracket fixed to the house or on a firm, level base, so it stays dry and stable.

Damp air indoors is common in older homes near water. A heat pump keeps rooms evenly warm, which reduces condensation, and dry mode helps on mild damp days. See [heat pump dehumidification](/heat-pump-dehumidification/).

## Heat pump installation in an older Brooklands house

Heat pump installation in an older Brooklands house means sizing for timber walls with limited insulation. These houses lose heat faster, especially when the wind is up, so they need a unit sized for the coldest mornings. We check the insulation and windows, measure the rooms and work out the capacity from there. If the house has a separate living room and kitchen, two smaller units can heat it better than one large one.

## Brooklands bedrooms and sleepouts

Some Brooklands properties have a sleepout, a studio or a bedroom wing that's well away from the lounge. Each of those is better served by its own small unit than by turning up a lounge unit and hoping heat travels. A [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/) can run them from one outdoor unit if they're close enough together.
