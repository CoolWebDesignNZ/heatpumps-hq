## Heat pumps for McLeans Island rural homes

On a McLeans Island property, three things decide the heat pump. There's the size and layout of the house. There's how exposed it is to wind and frost. And there's whether it has to take over from a log burner. McLeans Island is mostly open, rural land out towards the Waimakariri River. Homes sit on lifestyle blocks and farms, not suburban sections.

- **Bigger houses.** Homes here are often large, with the rooms spread out. One wall unit can't reach them all. The choice is usually ducted heating or several wall units, as set out below.
- **Exposure.** Houses take the full weather coming across the plains. Wind pulls heat out through the walls and windows. Frost lowers a heat pump's output. Both shape the sizing and the outdoor unit, as covered below.
- **Placement.** Neighbours are far away, so boundary noise rarely decides it. Shelter from the nor'west, frost and stock decide where the outdoor unit goes.

## Heat pump installation for a bigger McLeans Island house

Say your living areas are at one end and the bedrooms at the other. One wall unit will struggle to heat that. The options we usually price are:

- **[Ducted heating](/ducted-heat-pumps-christchurch/).** A vent in every room, with the indoor unit in the roof space. Good for warmth through the whole house, with nothing on the walls.
- **Two or three wall units** in the rooms you use every day. Each runs on its own outdoor unit, or on a [multi-room system](/multi-room-heat-pump-installation/).

We size from each room, the insulation, the windows and how you use the house. On an exposed site, we size for frosty mornings, not mild afternoons. A heat pump's output drops as the outside air gets colder.

## Wind, frost and the outdoor unit on McLeans Island

On a rural property, the outdoor unit should sit out of the prevailing nor'west. It needs a raised base, well clear of the ground. Frost and long grass can block the airflow. Defrost water needs to drain away from paths. Stock and machinery are another reason to choose the spot with care. A small fence or a wall bracket keeps the unit out of harm's way.

## Heat pump or log burner on McLeans Island

A heat pump and a log burner each do something the other can't. Lots of rural homes out here already have a burner. A heat pump needs no firewood carted, stacked or lit. It heats on a timer before you get up. It can also cool the house on hot nor'west days. A burner keeps going through a power cut. Some McLeans Island owners keep both. The heat pump does the everyday heating, and the burner covers the coldest nights. We'll size the heat pump to carry the house on its own.
