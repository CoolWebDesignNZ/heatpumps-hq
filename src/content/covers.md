## What a cover is for

People put covers on outdoor units for a few reasons:

- **Looks.** A slatted screen hides a grey box on a deck, patio or front path.
- **Sun shelter.** A roof over a unit on a hot north or west wall keeps direct sun off the casing in summer.
- **Protection from above.** Shelter from things dropping onto the unit, such as roof run-off where there's no spouting, falling branches or snow sliding off a roof.
- **Keeping pets and children away from the fan guard.**

## What a cover doesn't do

A cover isn't needed to protect the unit from ordinary weather, because outdoor units are built to be outside. It won't make it more efficient in winter. It won't fix noise from a worn fan or a unit that's vibrating on loose feet. And it won't stop frost, which forms on the coil from moisture in the air the unit pulls through.

## The rule that matters: airflow

Most domestic outdoor units pull outside air in through the coil at the back and sides and push it out the front. In heating mode it pulls heat out of that air. Anything that slows the air down makes the unit work harder for the same heat.

A cover that blocks airflow can cause:

- **Recirculation.** Cold air blown out the front bounces back off the cover and gets drawn straight back through the coil. The unit is heating with colder air than it should, and loses output.
- **More icing.** Colder air across the coil means more frost and more defrost cycles, which means less time heating your house.
- **Trapped defrost water.** A cover with a solid base or a tight frame can stop meltwater draining, and that water can refreeze around the base.
- **More noise.** Sound reflects off solid panels close to the unit.

So a safe cover:

1. **Leaves the front fan outlet open.** No panel or slats directly in front of the fan.
2. **Leaves room at the back and sides.** On most units that's where the air comes in. Your unit's installation manual sets the minimum clearances for that model.
3. **Has an open base.** Water must be able to drain and fall away from the unit.
4. **Can be removed for servicing.** A technician needs to reach the panels, the service valves and the electrical cover.
5. **Is fixed down properly.** A loose screen flapping in a nor'wester is noisy and can damage the unit.

Full wraps, plastic covers sold for storage, and tarps don't belong on a running heat pump.

## Covers at install

The best time to think about a cover is before the unit goes in. When we plan an [installation](/heat-pump-installation-in-christchurch/), we choose a spot for the outdoor unit that suits airflow, drainage, noise at the boundary and how it will look. Sometimes a better position means you don't need a cover at all. If you do want one, we can tell you how much space to leave and which designs suit that spot.

If the unit is near a neighbour, placement matters for noise as much as looks. Our [boundary rules guide](/heat-pump-boundary-rules-consent-nz/) explains the limits that apply.
